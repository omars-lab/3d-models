Every after-print question is a short procedure: one line on what is checked, the pieces by carved id, numbered one-action steps, what each result looks like, then the question. No recap, no filler. The format, with do and don't examples, is `.claude/skills/review-print/asking.md` (#659). Read it before each round.

**Why:** Omar, 2026-10-09, on spl-1 row 4: "not clear and straighformward and in simple sop format ... easy to get a mistake". The re-ask then named uppers KT, LT, DT whose letters were hidden inside pairs closed by earlier rows ("what lt and dt peices?"). Asking for the closed pairs by the lower's id got a one-pick answer.

**Too many questions (Omar, 2026-10-23, split-05):** he turned down a six-question round with "too many questions", then answered in one line: "both are good". Ask for one free-text verdict first ("how did it come out?"). Ask a follow-up only for a row his words leave open that the next plate depends on, and at most one or two. Record his words as the answer to every row they cover.

**How to apply:** name pieces by the id still visible after earlier rows. Split a row with several checks into several questions in one call. Observation options get one line on what each changes, not pros and cons. Builds on [[questions-name-carved-ids]].
