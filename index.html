§ 02

The Orbs

Not cutters — ornaments. Each orb is a few lines of pattern grammar in bikar, a text DSL for Islamic geometry, compiled straight to a watertight printable mesh: star patterns inscribed on a polyhedron's faces, projected to the sphere, and solidified into struts or woven ribbons. Every orb is adjustable — open one in the Orb Lab to dial radius, star angles, and struts, then download your own STL.

§ 03

The Bricks

The same bikar grammar, pointed at a studded brick instead of a sphere — LEGO-compatible pieces with the geometry carved into the top face. Two gates judge every one before it becomes an STL: whether the mesh is printable, and whether the piece will actually hold — enough anchors, enough studs engaged, no rotation about a lone tube. Open one in the Lego Lab to dial the geometry and the clutch.
A caution worth stating plainly: nobody has published caliper data on a printed stud, so every clutch dimension here is an unmeasured default — a starting point, not a measurement. The Lab says so on each value, and printing a coupon is how you replace one with a number.

§ 04

The Coasters

The same bikar grammar laid flat. Each of these began as a GeoGebra construction video — a step-by-step Islamic-geometry walkthrough — traced into pattern grammar and then debossed into the top face of a printable disc, so the drawing becomes something you can set a cup on. Every coaster is one knob deep: size sets the finished diameter, and the inscribed art rescales with it, so the same source renders a 40 mm mini or a 90 mm standard — never a mesh scaled after the fact, always re-drawn at the new size. The mesh gate is the whole automated check here; a printed coaster is what turns the relief depth into a measurement. Open one in the Coaster Lab to size it for the printer in the room and watch every structural check re-run as you move the knob. Two of the plates are interlocked (D-069): every straight edge carries a self-mating dovetail, so tiles of the same size plug together into a runner, and the preview shows a mated pair rather than one tile. Two more are minimal (D-070): no slab at all — the strap network of the construction is the whole coaster, its open cells are holes, and every top edge is rounded over, so the cup sits on the pattern itself. And two are bordered (D-071): a second pattern laid in a band that follows the outline, the field construction inset behind it, so one coaster carries two patterns in one relief.
