## Counter-evidence deep dives

### 1. The novelty claim — MCAD ships assembly-time dimensional clearance checks

The hardest attack available, and it partially lands:

- **SolidWorks Clearance Verification** — official help (https://help.solidworks.com/2022/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_Clearance_Verification_SWassy.htm, 403 to bot; search snippet): "checks the minimum distance between the components and reports clearances that fail to meet the minimum acceptable clearance you specify." Confirmed by fetched secondary source https://blog.exbuild.com/old_blog/how-to-check-for-clearance-verification-in-solidworks: "By specifying the minimum acceptable clearance around your components you can pinpoint areas in your assembly that may require extra attention." This **is** a dimensional clearance validation against a user-declared value at assembly level. What it is not: automatic at mate time, and it computes clearance from geometry rather than comparing declared connector contracts. SolidWorks also ships Interference Detection and Dynamic Clearance (https://my.solidworks.com/training/master/194/clearance-detection-interference-detection, https://hawkridgesys.com/blog/solidworks-clear-assemblies-using-dynamic-clearance — unverified snippets).
- **Onshape Interference Detection** — official help exists (https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/View/interference_detection.htm, unverified snippet: "detects and views any interference in a Part Studio or Assembly… interference is shown in red"), shipped since at least release 1.201 (https://www.onshape.com/en/resource-center/what-is-new/configuration-inputs-assembly-interference-detection-panoramic-stereo-rendering, fetched). The C2 doc's "OnShape's error is solver degrees-of-freedom" under-represents Onshape. Crucially though, a clearance-threshold version was **requested and not shipped** (https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/15048/..., unverified snippet) — which actually supports the narrowed claim.
- **NopSCADlib** (not in the survey's set) — fetched README: screws, inserts, clearance-hole modules, BOM generation, exploded assembly views — and **no** fit validation, no error when a screw doesn't fit a hole. Supports the claim; should be cited as surveyed-and-negative.
- **Zoo/KCL** — no joint/connector system with dimension validation found; nearest feature is user-authored geometry assertions ("check that your part fulfills its requirements", https://zoo.dev/docs/kcl-book/checking.html, unverified snippet) — manual, not connect-time. IceSL/Curv/Fornjot: no assembly/joint dimension validation surfaced in searches (absence of evidence, noted as coverage limit).
- **build123d** — refutation attempt failed: fetched joints.py contains only `TypeError("other must of type RigidJoint not {type(other)}")`-style kind checks and `ValueError` range checks on angle/position; the model answered explicitly that radius/diameter appear only in visualization `symbol` properties.

Net: the claim survives **only** in the form "no surveyed system validates a *declared* connector contract *at connect/compile time*"; "validates connector dimensions at assembly time" simpliciter is false for MCAD (SolidWorks Clearance Verification is exactly a user-specified minimum-clearance dimensional check on demand).

### 2. Fit-intent ladder — the spread is real and wide

- https://tools.creative3dp.com/blog/press-fit-tolerances-3d-printing/ (fetched): the ladder is transcribed **exactly** (−0.10/+0.05/+0.15/+0.35 diametral, rigid filaments, 5–25 mm range), and the source itself demands the separation the doc adopts ("hole in CAD = nominal + fit gap + hole compensation (+0.24mm-ish)") plus a "10-minute coupon". Provenance is clean — but it remains a single vendor-blog source.
- https://www.aon3d.com/applications/engineering-fits-how-to-design-for-3d-printed-assemblies/ (fetched): "allow a clearance of 1–2× your extrusion width" (~0.75–1.5 mm with a 0.6 mm nozzle); ISO 286 "mostly irrelevant for FDM." That is 5–10× the ladder's sliding/free gaps.
- https://www.hydraresearch3d.com/design-rules (fetched): "~0.2 mm for loose fit ~0.1 mm for tight fit" (per-side ⇒ 0.2/0.4 diametral) — 2–4× the ladder's snug/sliding.
- https://3dprintcalcs.uk/reference/common-tolerances/ (unverified snippet): press 0.1, sliding 0.2–0.3, loose 0.4–0.5 mm **per side** — again ~2–4× diametrally.
- https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/11/10/2810 (unverified snippet, peer-reviewed): all ISO 286 tolerance zones for holes/shafts achievable on a calibrated FDM printer (hole horizontal expansion 0.13 mm in their setup) — this *supports* the doc's calibrated-compensation architecture against AON3D's pessimism.
- Internal math: with symmetric closed windows of ±0.05, the snug and sliding acceptance intervals meet at gap = 0.10 mm (|0.10−0.05| = 0.05 passes snug AND |0.10−0.15| = 0.05 passes sliding). "Adjacent fit classes can never overlap" is false at the boundary; and 0.05 is half the *smallest* step, not "half a ladder step" generally.

### 3. PrusaSlicer hole compensation — still true, but date-stamp it

- Fetched https://github.com/prusa3d/PrusaSlicer/issues/1065: "Hole size compensation, was: XY Size Compensation the wrong way round?", opened July 2018, **still open**. Note: it began as an inverted-sign bug report, not a pure feature request — the doc's label "hole-compensation request #1065" is a slight simplification.
- Fetched https://github.com/prusa3d/PrusaSlicer/issues/14953: opened Oct 29, 2025, **open**, requests the split, cites ideaMaker's separate hole/contour settings as prior art. Also open: #4561, #5145, #10822, #13815, #14468 (unverified snippets).
- Release check: stable is 2.9.4 (Nov 2025), with 2.9.5/2.9.6 in 2026 (SLA pipeline, ColorMix — no hole compensation mentioned) and **PrusaSlicer 3.0 announced as weeks away** (https://all3dp.com/6/is-prusaslicer-3-0-coming-soon-..., unverified snippet). The claim is load-bearing and could flip within C2's implementation window; even then, compiler-side compensation would remain the only cross-slicer path, which is the actual argument.

### 4. Slicer position-loss — the steelman partially wins

All three slicers **do** have import workflows that preserve relative positions: PrusaSlicer's "load these files as a single object with multiple parts" dialog (https://help.prusa3d.com/article/importing-multi-material-model_121191, fetched — dialog confirmed, though the KB doesn't state position preservation explicitly; the survey's forum sources do), Bambu Studio's Assembly View which "remembers assembled placements even after you split to objects for plating" (survey-cited wiki, fetch blocked), and Cura's select-all → merge-models workaround (survey-cited, unverified snippet). All require parts exported in a shared world frame and user interaction. The doc's §3 summary ("assembled placement does not survive import") states as absolute what its own survey §1 documents as conditional. The design conclusion — per-part print-oriented STL at own origin, assembled intent belongs to 3MF — is still the right call, since the preserving workflows are interactive, fragile (CLI can't answer the dialog, PrusaSlicer #11474), and defeat per-part print orientation anyway.

### 5. BOSL2 spin sign — more documented than claimed

Fetched https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/attachments.scad: (a) attach()'s `spin=` is documented as "Angle to rotate the child around **the axis of the parent anchor**"; (b) the wiki's Spin subsection documents the library-wide convention: "Specifying a spin angle when creating an object rotates the object **counter-clockwise around the Z axis** by the given number of degrees." Composing the two gives a plausible documented answer (CCW about the parent anchor vector). What remains genuinely undocumented: whether attach()'s spin follows that library-wide CCW convention about the *parent's* vector (vs the mated child axis, which is anti-parallel — the exact ambiguity bikar §4.4 resolves). The "hard to predict" quote is verified verbatim ("…not the same as the spin argument to the child, which will usually rotate around some other direction that may be hard to predict"). The doc should say "the composed sign for attach() is never explicitly stated" rather than "sign convention is undocumented."

### 6. Deterministic tree vs solver — the FreeCAD evidence is weaker than presented, and the solver side has a better story

- Fetched https://github.com/FreeCAD/FreeCAD/issues/20377: title verbatim ("Assembly: Solver goes unstable and blows up assembled parts") — but it was **closed as not planned**, and the trigger was a document *corrupted by experimenting with Assembly3/Assembly4 workbenches in the same file*, not dimensionally inconsistent joint declarations. Citing it as the solver's response "on inconsistent input" (doc §3) overreaches.
- Solver steelman: SolveSpace detects redundant/inconsistent constraint sets, turns the canvas red, and "determines which constraints could be removed to fix the problem" (https://solvespace.com/ref.pl, https://solvespace.github.io/solvespace-web/constraints.html — unverified snippets) — i.e., a mature solver gives *diagnosis*, not blowups. Onshape's over-defined-mate error is likewise a graceful refusal. Counter-counter: SolveSpace's own docs note the "allow redundant constraints" mode "makes the solver less stable" (unverified snippet), which supports the doc's underlying instinct that redundancy and numeric solving interact badly.

### 7. CNC Kitchen strength ratios — verified, but they are one lab's hook-bending numbers

Fetched article confirms exactly: PLA hooks printed standing failed at 40 kg = **55%** of lying-printed; PETG **46%**; ASA **29%** (with the caveat that ASA improves ~20% in an enclosure). Two complications for §6's use of them: (a) the test is a hook under load — bending/mixed-mode, not the pin-shear case §6 reasons about; (b) other measured sources span a wide range: solid PLA Z-strength only 33% below X (~67% retained) in one cited study (https://www.snapmaker.com/blog/how-strong-are-3d-printed-parts/, unverified snippet) vs "4–5× stronger in XY" (~20–25% retained) claims elsewhere (https://rapidmade.com/isotropic-vs-anisotropic-strength-in-3d-printing/, unverified snippet). The doc's use (motivating a coupon, not a spec) is compatible with the spread, but the numbers should be labeled "one measured configuration," not constants.

### 8. 3MF spec — verified verbatim

Fetched raw spec: matrices "have the form 'm00 m01 m02 m10 m11 m12 m20 m21 m22 m30 m31 m32' where each value is a decimal number" (12 values, row-major, last three = translation); components: "A 3D manufacturing device MUST respect the relative positions of the component objects; it MUST NOT transform them relative to each other except as specified in the document"; negative determinant: "the resultant shape MUST NOT change the sign of its volume." All three doc/survey claims check out. Minor: the survey's paraphrase of `partnumber` semantics ("preserve across edits, duplicate on split, remove on merge") was not confirmed in my targeted fetch, which surfaced a different sentence — treat that detail as unverified.

### 9. Bambu coupon methodology — corroborated, primary blocked

wiki.bambulab.com returns HTTP 402 to fetchers (both target pages). The undersize÷2 per-side rule is corroborated independently: search snippet of the wiki itself ("If a hole is measured to be 0.5 mm too small, a suitable size hole can be printed with compensation of +0.25 mm"), the calibration-model formula "X-Y hole compensation = (18 − measured)/2" (https://makerworld.com/en/models/700182-..., unverified snippet), and a Bambu forum statement "if the diameter dimension is 0.1mm too small, 0.05 must be entered" (https://forum.bambulab.com/t/adjusting-the-hole-diameter/69468, unverified snippet). Hole-vs-contour separation also corroborated. No contradiction found.

## Misgrounded or missing citations

1. **§3 novelty bullet under-represents Onshape and omits MCAD clearance tools.** "OnShape's error is solver degrees-of-freedom" reads as "that's all Onshape has"; Onshape ships on-demand Interference Detection (official help page), and SolidWorks ships Clearance Verification — a user-specified minimum-clearance dimensional check. Neither is connect-time or contract-based, but both belong in the survey as the nearest prior art. The survey's §3 "No DSL found that errors on geometric mismatch of connectors" is fine; the doc's restatement "No surveyed system validates connector dimensions at connect time" leans on "surveyed" excluding all of MCAD's analysis tooling without saying so.
2. **FreeCAD #20377 is cited for a proposition it doesn't support** ("solver blowups on inconsistent input" → actually cross-workbench corruption, closed not-planned). Keep the quote, fix the attribution of cause; #27557 (DOF miscount) is the better-fitting citation and is already listed.
3. **BOSL2 spin claim over-broad** — see deep dive 5; the honest formulation is "the composed sign of attach()'s spin is never explicitly stated (the library-wide spin convention is documented as CCW about Z, and attach() names the parent-anchor axis, but the two are never composed for the anti-aligned case)."
4. **§3 slicer bullet drops its own survey's qualifiers** — "all drop imported objects to the bed and/or re-arrange them" should be "by default / unless the user opts into the multipart-import or merge workflows, which require a shared world frame and manual interaction."
5. **Missing surveyed-and-negative entries that would strengthen the novelty claim**: NopSCADlib (fasteners + BOM + exploded views, no fit validation — fetched README) and KCL's user-authored assertion checks (https://zoo.dev/docs/kcl-book/checking.html). Both were in the audit brief's attack list and both came back supporting bikar; cite them so the next auditor doesn't have to re-run the search.
6. **±0.05 window prose is mathematically wrong at the boundary** (shared endpoint at gap 0.10; "half a ladder step" only true of the smallest step). Not a citation issue but a spec bug in §5 / validator #6.
7. **Minor**: §10 row 10 says "1e-6 mm / 1e-6 rad" while §4.3 promises the error reports "twist in degrees" — pick one unit story. The 3MF `partnumber` paraphrase in survey §1 is unverified in detail.

## Recommended doc changes

**(a) Narrow the novelty claim (§3 bullet 3 and Appendix A) — required.** Suggested drop-in paragraph:

> Mainstream MCAD does validate dimensional clearance at assembly level — just not at connect time and not from declared contracts. SolidWorks' Clearance Verification "checks the minimum distance between the components and reports clearances that fail to meet the minimum acceptable clearance you specify" ([SolidWorks help](https://help.solidworks.com/2022/english/SolidWorks/sldworks/c_Clearance_Verification_SWassy.htm)), and Onshape ships on-demand [Interference Detection](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/View/interference_detection.htm) (a clearance-threshold variant remains a [forum request](https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/15048/add-an-offset-to-check-interference-for-quickly-checking-clearance-tolerances)). These are geometric analyses a user runs after assembly, computed from meshes/B-rep — they know nothing about intent (a press fit *should* interfere; Clearance Verification can only call that a violation). What no surveyed system does — including NopSCADlib's fastener library (clearance-hole modules, BOM, exploded views, [no fit validation](https://github.com/nophead/NopSCADlib)) and KCL's user-authored [assertion checks](https://zoo.dev/docs/kcl-book/checking.html) — is compare *declared* connector dimensions plus a *declared* fit intent at connect/compile time and fail the build. That narrower composition remains novel within everything surveyed.

**(b) Requalify the BOSL2 spin gap (§3 bullet 2, §4.4).** Suggested replacement sentence:

> BOSL2 documents its library-wide spin as "counter-clockwise around the Z axis" and attach()'s `spin=` as rotating "around the axis of the parent anchor" ([attachments.scad](https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/attachments.scad)), but never composes the two for the anti-aligned attach case — whether positive spin is CCW about the parent's or the (opposite) child's mate axis is left to experiment, and the same reference concedes the child-spin interaction "may be hard to predict." C2 states the composed sign outright and pins it with a unit test.

**(c) Qualify the slicer-import claim (§3 bullet 1).** Change "PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio, and Cura all drop imported objects to the bed and/or re-arrange them" to "…all drop or re-arrange *independently imported* objects by default; each has an interactive preserve-positions workflow (PrusaSlicer's multipart-object dialog, Bambu's Assembly View, Cura's merge-models), but all require a shared world frame, manual interaction the CLI cannot script (PrusaSlicer #11474), and give up per-part print orientation — so per-part print-oriented STL remains the honest export."

**(d) Fix the FreeCAD attribution (§3 bullet 3).** Replace "on inconsistent input" with: "FreeCAD 1.0's integrated solver can go 'unstable and blow up assembled parts' when its assembly state is perturbed ([#20377](https://github.com/FreeCAD/FreeCAD/issues/20377) — triggered by cross-workbench file corruption, closed not-planned) and miscounts DOF on valid input ([#27557](https://github.com/freecad/freecad/issues/27557))." Add the solver steelman as a divergence note:

> The steelman for solvers: mature ones fail gracefully — SolveSpace detects redundant/inconsistent constraint sets, flags them visually, and "determines which constraints could be removed to fix the problem" ([SolveSpace reference](https://solvespace.com/ref.pl)); Onshape's over-defined-mate error is a clean refusal, not a blowup. We diverge because bikar's assemblies are trees with at most closure checks — there is nothing to solve, so buying a solver would buy only its failure modes (initial-position sensitivity, redundancy instability — SolveSpace's own docs note its allow-redundant mode "makes the solver less stable") for a use case where the closed-form composition already answers every placement exactly.

**(e) Fix the ±0.05 window math (§5, validator #6).** Either make the check half-open (`≤ 0.05` on one side, `< 0.05` on the other), shrink the window to ±0.049, or explicitly state the tie-break at a shared boundary (a gap of exactly 0.10 mm satisfies both snug and sliding as written); and change "0.05 mm is half a ladder step" to "half the smallest ladder step (snug→sliding, 0.10 mm)".

**(f) Date-stamp the PrusaSlicer claim (§3, §5).** Add: "current as of PrusaSlicer 2.9.x, July 2026; the split remains an open request chain (#1065 since 2018, #14953 Oct 2025) with 3.0 imminent — re-verify at implementation time. Even if a future PrusaSlicer ships hole compensation, compiler-side application remains the only mechanism that works identically across all three slicers and keeps the declared/compensated split visible to the contract checker."

**(g) Label the CNC Kitchen numbers (§3, §6).** Add after the 55%/46% citation: "one lab's hook-load (bending) results; measured Z-retention across studies ranges from ~25% to ~67% depending on material, geometry, and test mode — the numbers motivate the coupon, they are not constants."
