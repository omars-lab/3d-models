## Counter-evidence deep dives

### Novelty (§1)
Attacked via 5 search framings (multi-piece baseplate mosaic, split pattern across plates, lithophane baseplates, MakerWorld image-to-brick, Etsy wall-art panels). Nothing published does printed-relief pieces + seam validation. Closest finds, none in the survey's §3 set:
- https://bricksly.net/tools/brickapic — (unverified snippet, fetch 403) "select either standard 32x32 plates or large 48x48 backings, and the preview will immediately re-render a grid overlay showing you exactly where the seams will sit" — splits one image across multiple plates *with explicit seam awareness*, but composes it from stock 1×1 parts and validates nothing.
- https://thebrickblogger.com/2025/04/add-mosaics-and-murals-to-your-lego-city/ — (fetched) EclipseBricks, BrickBuildersPro, PriBri sell "custom patterns, murals, and graffiti-style art on LEGO elements" — continuous images spanning multiple stock tiles via 2D printing on parts; no 3D-printed pieces, no relief, no registration validation.
- https://brickmosaicdesigner.com/en — (unverified snippet) "relief style" mosaics from multi-size stock plates ("amazing three-dimensional effect") — relief-by-stacking stock parts, not engraved relief on printed pieces.
- https://machineblocks.com — (fetched) individual-block generation only; "Perfect fit can be achieved with the Calibration Tool"; no mosaic/mural/multi-part feature found.
**Verdict: the claim stands as scoped (K2-compliant scoping is doing real work), but the near-miss list above should be cited, because Brickapic in particular does the "where do the seams sit" half of the problem.**

### 0.2 mm gap convention (§3.1)
- https://www.cailliau.org/... (fetched) — all four quoted claims confirmed verbatim, including "my measurements have consistently turned up the value of 0.1mm play on each side". One hedge stripped upstream: pitch is "7.993mm ±0.007mm or 8mm to within better than one hundredth of a mm" — the survey's "8.0 mm exactly" and summary-table "measured, exact" flatten this (minor K1 in the *survey*, inherited by doc §3.1's "measured independently").
- https://www.brickowl.com/help/stud-dimensions (fetched) — "the dimension will be 8x - 0.2mm"; "The 0.2mm is for tolerance on either side of the brick"; stud height 1.7 mm. Confirmed.
- https://www.bartneck.de/2019/04/21/lego-brick-dimensions-and-measurements/ (fetched) — "There is a 0.2mm gap between bricks next to each other" — a fourth independent corroboration, citable.
- Spread check: no credible source found giving a *different* moulded-LEGO play (0.1/side is unanimous across Cailliau/BrickOwl/latericius/Bartneck/StudyDaily-snippet). The spread that exists is in **printed**-brick practice: a Printables parametric design reportedly replaces "the normal LEGO brick clearance (0.1 mm)" with "ClearanceOutside (0.2 mm)" for FDM (model 368231 — 403, unverified snippet), and cfinke's LEGO.scad tunes stud fit per printer. This mildly complicates "a printed piece inherits a proven inter-part gap": FDM practice often doubles the moulded clearance precisely because of the elephant-foot/XY-error effects B.4 and B.1 name.

### Acuity chain (§3.1, B.3)
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8523788/ (fetched) — "The vernier threshold (smallest detectable offset) for humans is as low as 2 to 5 arcseconds"; foveal cone "about 30 s of arc"; "vernier acuity is 10-fold that of visual acuity". All three doc-side numbers confirmed. Caveats the doc does not carry: optimum requires Michelson contrast ≥0.22 (exponential threshold rise below), no crowding, ideal synchronous stimuli.
- Hecht & Mintz: search confirms "from about 10 minutes at the lowest illuminations to 0.5 second at the highest illuminations", J Gen Physiol 1939;22:593–612 (via Semantic Scholar entry https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/381605e7e9ce3157aac34b7a4a3e749b2711eb28 and citing literature — still not a fetched primary, but now multiply corroborated; can be upgraded from "snippet-only" to "corroborated secondary").
- Arithmetic all verified by hand: 0.2/450 rad = 91.7 arcsec ≈ 1.53 arcmin ✓; 2–5 arcsec at 450 mm = 4.4–10.9 µm ✓; 1 arcmin ≈ 0.131 mm ✓.
- **Steelman for invisibility**: H&M's 0.5 arcsec is a *high-contrast dark line on a bright field at high luminance*; the mural's seam is a gap between two matte, same-colour, top-rounded FDM surfaces — closer to a low-contrast shadow line, where H&M's own curve degrades toward 10 arcmin and PMC8523788's contrast dependence bites. The doc's bolded "will be visible" holds for a *shadowed groove crossing lit relief* (the actual geometry: relief edges cast shadows into a 0.2 mm slot, so contrast is plausibly high) but is not guaranteed in flat blank-field regions. A conditional is warranted, and it actually *helps* B.3's fallback story.

### FDM XY accuracy (B.1)
- https://jlc3dp.com/blog/accuracy-prototyping (fetched) — "XY Accuracy: ±0.1 mm to ±0.3 mm", "Wall Tolerances: ±0.2 mm typically" confirmed; the page presents these generically and never distinguishes desktop vs industrial (the survey already flags its placement of tuned printers as own judgment — correctly).
- Counter: forum.bambulab.com "How to tune your Bambu printer for dimensional accuracy" (t/25564) and "Dimensional Accuracy Calibration" (t/112292) — (unverified snippets) users report holding ±0.05 mm after per-filament flow calibration; one P1S user "spot on" at defaults; the same forum also hosts a 6-page "Dimensionally Inaccurate Parts" thread (t/47984), so the class is bimodal, not uniformly good. Net: B.1's conclusion (jog detectable) survives even the best case — 50 µm ≥ 5× the 10 µm vernier bound — but "one to two orders of magnitude" is the untuned-printer figure.

### Clone plates (B.2)
- https://myleniumsbrickcorner.wordpress.com/2018/11/10/lego-vs-mega-an-analysis-part-2/ (fetched) — the most promising-looking "analysis" source explicitly contains **no numeric measurements**; qualitative only ("a micrometer too little or too much can have an enormous impact"). The absence finding is *strengthened*: I also failed to find caliper pitch data for Mega/Gobricks/Brickyard/Strictly Briks across a span. CAL-CLB-01 remains the only path to the datum. (Unexhausted: Mylenium part 1; rseater's MIT clone-comparison link list.)

### Elephant's foot (B.4)
- https://help.prusa3d.com/article/elephant-foot-compensation_114487 (fetched) — confirms "squished against the heated print bed… usually a bit wider than it should be" and "Values around 0.2 mm usually work well for the default 0.4 mm nozzle". **It contains no per-side bulge magnitude.** The survey's "the uncompensated first-layer bulge is itself in the 0.1–0.2 mm-per-side class" is an *inference from the compensation default*, and doc B.4's "(0.1–0.2 mm per side, same source)" presents that inference as sourced — a K1-class over-attribution.
- Measured examples found (unverified blog snippets): 20 mm cube measuring 20.5 mm at the base (~0.25 mm/side, https://www.3dmag.com/3d-wikipedia/elephant-foot-3d-printing-causes-and-fixes/); a plate 49.97 mm with 50.60 mm foot (~0.31 mm/side, via Creality/forum sources). These *exceed* the doc's stated class — the bet's failure mode (bulge closes the whole gap and binds) is more, not less, credible.

## Internal-consistency (K7) findings

1. **§4 worked-example span is wrong — must fix.** "The worked default `4 x 4 of 4 x 4` spans 32×32 studs = 256 mm — exactly one 32×32 baseplate." By §3.1's own formula, 8·c·f = 8·4·4 = **128 mm = 16×16 studs — one quadrant of a 32×32 baseplate**. Everything else in the doc assumes 16 pieces of 4×4 studs (V14's "nine tube candidates" = 3×3 ✓ for a 4×4-stud piece; V17's "16 rows, 16 STLs" ✓; "each piece 31.8 × 31.8 mm" ✓ = 4·8−0.2), so the minimal fix is correcting the span sentence, not the declaration. To actually fill a 32×32 plate: `4 x 4 of 8 x 8` (16 pieces, 63.8 mm each — V14's tube count becomes 49) or `8 x 8 of 4 x 4` (64 pieces — breaks V17's example). This is the taxonomy's "all six presets" class.
2. **D-008 is already assigned — must fix.** Doc header and §4 say D-008 will record the cut rule and slivers/blanks policy; `docs/decisions-log.md` line 519 already holds "D-008 — W-F1 is a new clip-in-notch coupon" and the log runs through D-012. The mural decision would be **D-013**.
3. **LG-P1/LG-P2 do not exist in `.claude/skills/prototype/catalog.md`** (K9-adjacent). `bets.md` lines 33–34 name them as settling coupons; the survey's provenance header claims it feeds "coupons LG-P1 / LG-P2 in .claude/skills/prototype/catalog.md"; the catalog's LG-series has only LG-F1/LG-F2. 4. **Everything else checks clean.** Verified against code/repo: `PART_RELIEF_MM = 0.2` at `bikar:packages/core/src/kernel3d/lego.ts:67`, `footprintMm` at `lego.ts:112` ✓; `SNAP_THRESHOLD_MM = 0.05` at `grid-gate.ts:110` ✓; `BRICK_MIN_FEATURE_MM = 0.7` at `brick.ts:91`, and relief depth **is** a min-feature candidate (`brick.ts:816–820`: `{ mm: spec.reliefDepthMm, what: 'relief depth' }` plus 'ceiling under the relief'), so 0.6 refuses and 0.8 clears ✓; commits `834dfc2` and `54615ff` exist on `feat/edge-to-edge-relief` with the decision doc `bikar:docs/decisions/2026-08-02-edge-to-edge-relief-top-face-arrangement.md` ✓; `brick-top-face.ts` exports `pocketLeavesRing` and `partitionTopFace`, documents the four face classes, the weld-clearance guard, and the rel-1e-6 area invariant (`brick-top-face.ts:481`) ✓; §7's blank-stripe arithmetic (1.5+0.1)×2+0.2 = 3.4 ✓; V15's ledger 4809.86+1.34 = 4811.20 ✓; V16's float32(12.8) = 12.800000190734863 ✓; lego-lab §1 non-goal quote (line 78) and §7.2 "2D boolean" correction (line 742, verified at `6b38342`) both accurately characterized ✓; survey § cross-references (§1.1, §1.3, §2.2, §3, §4) all section-accurate ✓; status line and Appendix B agree, and bets.md shows both CAL ids open with no measured entry, matching §3.3 ✓. ## Misgrounded or missing citations

- **B.4 bulge magnitude misattributed** (see deep dive): "(0.1–0.2 mm per side, same source)" — the Prusa page quantifies only the *compensation*, never the bulge. Reword as inference, or cite measured examples as snippet-class.
- **Survey §1.3 / summary table "8.0 mm exactly"** strips Cailliau's 7.993±0.007 hedge (K1, minor — Cailliau himself rounds to 8 mm "to within better than one hundredth"; doc §3.3's "better than enthusiast-caliper resolution" is actually the correct reading).
- **Hecht & Mintz can be upgraded**: full citation J Gen Physiol 1939;22(5):593–612; the 10 arcmin → 0.5 arcsec illumination range is now corroborated by multiple independent secondaries (still no fetched primary — keep the snippet flag but note corroboration).
- **Missing near-miss prior art** in survey §3: Brickapic (bricksly.net) and the printed-mural-on-stock-tiles customizers (EclipseBricks et al. via thebrickblogger 2025) — both split one image across seam lines and belong in the surveyed set the K2 scoping enumerates.

4. **(Must, misattribution)** B.4: replace "(0.1–0.2 mm per side, same source)" with: "the Prusa page does not quantify the bulge; we infer its order from the 0.2 mm compensation default that removes it, and community caliper examples (unfetched blog measurements: ~0.25–0.3 mm per side on uncompensated prints) suggest it can run *larger* than our whole designed gap."
5. **Divergence paragraph — novelty (paste into §1):**
   > Two near-misses found after the survey sharpen rather than defeat the gap claim. Brickapic (bricksly.net) splits one mosaic image across multiple 32×32/48×48 baseplates and "immediately re-render[s] a grid overlay showing you exactly where the seams will sit" — seam-aware panelization, but of stock 1×1 parts, with no printed pieces, no relief, and nothing validated. Custom-print vendors (EclipseBricks, BrickBuildersPro, PriBri) sell continuous mural images spanning multiple stock tiles — pattern continuity across seams, but by 2D printing on moulded parts. Both do half of LG-P1's job; neither does the half this doc exists for: engraved relief on printed bodies whose seam registration is checked, not hoped.
6. **Divergence paragraph — FDM accuracy (paste into B.1):**
   > Tuned-printer counter-evidence: Bambu-forum calibration threads report holding ±0.05 mm XY after per-filament flow calibration, well inside JLC's ±0.1–0.3 mm band — the band is a service-bureau envelope, not a floor. This narrows but does not overturn the bet: even ±0.05 mm per piece yields relative seam jogs of 50–100 µm, five to ten times the 4–11 µm vernier-detection bound, so a detectable jog remains the expected outcome on the best available printer; "one to two orders of magnitude" holds only for uncalibrated machines and should be read as the worst case.
7. **Qualifier — seam visibility (§3.1):** condition the bolded claim: "The seam **will be visible** *where it crosses lit relief* — a 0.2 mm slot between raised edges self-shadows into exactly the high-contrast dark line Hecht & Mintz measured. In flat matte blank-field regions the sourced thresholds do not directly apply (PMC8523788: optimal vernier/detection performance requires Michelson contrast ≥0.22; both acuity sources use high-contrast ideal stimuli), so there the seam may read fainter — which costs the design nothing, since those are the regions with no art to interrupt."
8. **Survey touch-ups:** §1.3 "8.0 mm exactly" → carry Cailliau's "7.993 ± 0.007 mm, i.e. 8 mm to within better than 0.01 mm"; add bartneck.de as a fourth gap corroborator; upgrade Hecht & Mintz to "corroborated secondary, primary unfetched" with the full J Gen Physiol citation; add Brickapic + printed-mural vendors to §3's enumerated set.
9. **Minor:** pin or drop the "328 `.bkr` files" count in §4 (time-varying, unpinned); no empirical residue found outside CAL-REG-01/CAL-CLB-01 — B.3 (aesthetic judgment) and B.4 (folded into LG-P1) are correctly bet-covered.
