## Counter-evidence deep dives

### 1. The no-CSG core bet — steelman: "just use Manifold everywhere from day one"

- https://lists.openscad.org/empathy/thread/TMJEJCZINIJNYJX2YF7IDNBAPQY66KIF — Marius Kintel, 2025-08-17: Manifold becomes OpenSCAD's **default** backend "after a long time of battle testing." The flagship code-CAD system whose entire modeling paradigm is 3D booleans now treats them as fast and reliable enough for everyone by default.
- https://lists.openscad.org/empathy/thread/D6KV3ZLXHLBHSITSQ5GPUZUKHURU4ABE — Manifold "no longer experimental" as of 2024-09-28 (CGAL still default at that point).
- https://github.com/openscad/openscad/pull/4533 — merged March 2023; "5-30x speedups over fast-csg (YMMV)"; CSG.scad 50 s → 0.8 s; a BOSL2 minkowski 4m31s → ~4 s.
- https://github.com/elalish/manifold/wiki/Manifold-Library — "guaranteed manifold output": the historical robustness argument against mesh CSG (non-manifold garbage output) is specifically what Manifold eliminates by construction.

What this does to the bet: the two classic justifications for avoiding 3D CSG — slow and fragile — are gone in 2026. The steelman says: adopt manifold-3d (2.8 MB, TS types, verified) on day one, model every hole/union as a boolean, and delete the z-band machinery, the ring-stitching surgery, and the §8 tripwire. What the steelman does NOT rebut: (a) bikar's print-validation gate needs a robust per-layer 2D region layer anyway, so the 2D boolean investment is not avoidable; (b) constructive meshes carry semantic provenance (which wall belongs to which strut/port) that a boolean output loses; (c) Manifold's own docs concede degenerate sliver triangles below ε may survive and error compounds across chained ops (survey §4, consistent with the wiki). The bet survives, but its stated rationale should shift from "booleans are risky" (no longer credible) to "we need the 2D layer regardless, and we need labeled geometry" — see recommended text below.

### 2. The one-verb/no-solver bet

- https://juraph.com/kiwi/playing_with_build123d/ — verified: "In CQ, parts are connected to assemblies using a system of constraints. This made placing components in assemblies far more difficult than I feel it should have been" and build123d joints let you "simply declare: 'This part goes right here', no fuss." Also notes the joint system is "not 100% yet." Supports the bet.
- https://forum.onshape.com/discussion/18037/mate-overdefines-the-assembly — even Onshape's one-mate-per-connection model hits "Mate over-defines assembly" the moment mates form a closed loop; users must manually degrade mates (revolute → cylindrical) to free DOF. Deterministic pairwise composition does not escape this: it just moves the failure to inconsistent derived placements.
- https://www.mathworks.com/help/robotics/ug/solve-inverse-kinematics-for-closed-loop-linkages.html — closed chains cannot be represented as a pure tree of pairwise joints; loop closure requires constraint solving. Out of scope for bikar's static v1 (no kinematics), but the *positional* analog is in scope: a 2×2 grid of tiles joined by pins is a connect-graph cycle, and the fourth `connect` either re-derives a placement the piece already has (must be checked for consistency within tolerance) or silently conflicts. The doc's only rule ("one connection per port") does not prevent this, because cycles use different ports of the same piece. The tile-wall sibling doc guarantees users hit this in C2/C3.
- Verdict: GROUNDED for the orb lamp (a two-piece open chain); CONTESTED for the tile wall. The doc needs a stated cycle rule (e.g., connect graph must be a tree; extra connects become *checks*, not placements) — Onshape's over-define error is the precedent.

### 3. The novelty claim — "no surveyed system ships dimensional contracts on connectors"

This is the hardest-hit claim. Dimensional contracts on mechanical features are, in fact, shipped widely:

- https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/screws.scad — **inside the very ecosystem the survey covers most deeply.** `screw()`/`nut()` take `tolerance=` ISO/ASME thread classes ("6g"/"6H", "1A/2A/3A", "1B/2B/3B"); `screw_hole()` takes named clearance fits "close"/"normal"/"loose" (+ ISO "fine"/"medium"/"coarse"), plus "tap"/"self tap"; `$slop` is a separate printer-compensation knob ("extra space left to account for printing over-extrusion"). That is: intent class + printer compensation, separated — the exact §5 structure the doc claims "every surveyed ecosystem conflates." The survey audited attachments.scad and distributors.scad but never opened screws.scad.
- https://www.javelin-tech.com/blog/2018/11/solidworks-hole-wizard-tolerances/ — SolidWorks Hole Wizard carries tolerance/fit specifications on the hole feature itself since SW2019, propagating to callouts; SolidWorks dimensions support ISO 286 fit classes (H7/g6 etc.) as first-class tolerance types (chart background: https://www.rivcut.com/resources/tolerance-fit-chart).
- https://help.solidworks.com/2025/english/solidworks/sldworks/c_TolAnalyst_Overview.htm — TolAnalyst performs assembly-level min/max worst-case and RSS stack-up validation over DimXpert-toleranced features — commercial CAD *does* validate dimensional contracts across an assembly, which is close kin to the doc's "contract check" gate.
- Fusion 360's Hole feature offers Close/Normal/Loose clearance classes per fastener standard (community confirmation: https://forums.autodesk.com/t5/fusion-design-validate-document/hole-feature-suggestion/td-p/12301100 — (unverified snippet); the underlying standard for clearance-hole series is ISO 273 / NF EN 20273: https://www.boutique.afnor.org/en-gb/standard/nf-en-20273/fasteners-clearance-hole-for-bolts-and-screws/fa021774/186).
- https://github.com/gridfinity-unofficial/specification/blob/main/README.md — Gridfinity bakes a dimensional contract into its interface spec (42 mm grid, 41.5 mm bins, 0.25 mm/side clearance); the whole ecosystem interoperates because the connector tolerance is specified, not because each author does fit arithmetic.
- https://community.multiboard.io/c/printer-settings/how-to-make-adjustments-to-a-various-parts-to-allow-for-tolerances — Multiboard parts ship with 0.25 mm design tolerance and a user-adjustable `MB_Connector_Tolerance` parameter (the doc already half-acknowledges this by saying "Multiboard-style user-adjustable tolerance").
- https://github.com/rcolyer/threads-scad — even small OpenSCAD threading libraries take a `tolerance` parameter on thread generation.
- Academic side: https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12772 (JoinABLe, CVPR 2022) treats parametric CAD *joints* as first-class learned data (Fusion 360 Gallery assembly dataset includes joints, contact surfaces, holes) — but carries no fit/clearance semantics; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3379337.3415899 (Tsugite) machine-checks joint validity. Neither refutes the *connect-time contract validation* part; both refute "nobody types mechanical interfaces."

Also note mechanical engineering's limits-and-fits system (ISO 286) simply *is* a dimensional contract language — H7/g6 encodes exactly "intent class compiled to a diametral gap" — and it is a century old. What genuinely remains novel in bikar's design, as far as this audit could establish: (1) the contract lives on the **mating port itself** (not on a hole feature or a thread generator) for *arbitrary* printed interfaces, and (2) `connect` **fails compilation** on kind/dimension/fit mismatch. No surveyed or discovered code-CAD system does that pairing. But "no surveyed system ships dimensional contracts on connectors" and "every surveyed ecosystem conflates intent and compensation" are both false as written.

### 4. The fit ladder numbers

- https://tools.creative3dp.com/blog/press-fit-tolerances-3d-printing/ — verified verbatim: −0.10 / +0.05 / +0.15 / +0.35 mm diametral; holes 0.1–0.3 mm undersize; shafts ~0.1 mm oversize; ~0.24 mm undersize on 5 mm PLA hole/0.4 mm nozzle; PETG "eats ~0.05 mm"; TPU 2× interference, 0.5–0.8 mm running; crush ribs ~0.2 mm. The ladder is a faithful transcription — of exactly **one** source.
- The spread across credible sources is large:
  - https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing/ — recommends **0.5 mm** clearance for FDM (0.3 mm for SLA/SLS/MJF). The doc cites this guide in Appendix A as if it supported the ladder; it actually recommends 3–10× the ladder's snug/sliding gaps.
  - https://www.snapmaker.com/blog/3d-printing-tolerances/ and https://sinterit.com/3d-printing-guide/design-for-3d-printing/tolerances-3d-printing/ — FDM mating clearance typically 0.4–0.6 mm; SLS/MJF 0.2–0.4 mm.
  - https://3dput.com/complete-guide-to-3d-printing-tolerances-and-fit-getting-perfect-clearance-for-moving-parts/ — press 0.1 mm/side (≈ +0.2 diametral *clearance*, i.e., opposite sign from the ladder's −0.10 interference press), sliding 0.2–0.3, loose 0.4–0.5; PLA holes shrink 0.2–0.4 mm, PETG 0.3–0.5 mm.
- Reconciliation: the ladder's small numbers are *designed gaps after hole compensation on a calibrated printer*; the 0.4–0.6 mm school quotes *raw nominal clearances with no compensation*. The doc's architecture (profile-applied compensation + fit coupon) is exactly the mechanism that makes small intent gaps workable — but the doc should say the ladder is one source's calibrated-printer numbers, that mainstream guidance is 2–10× looser, and that the coupon is what arbitrates. Note also the sign convention risk: some sources' "press fit 0.1 mm" means clearance, the ladder's means interference.

### 5. The z-band hole model

- Internal contradiction: §4.1's very first example, `hole grub radial 3.2 at height 9  # radial hole through the wall`, is a hole whose axis is perpendicular to z through a curved cylindrical wall. §4.2 defines holes as z-bands subtracting a region from the 2D section over a z-range — which cannot express a radial hole (each z-slice would need a different-width rectangular cut; the boundary is a 3D intersection curve). §8 itself classifies "angled hole through a curved shell" as forcing op (1), reserved for C4. The doc's flagship BulbCollar therefore cannot be built with the machinery the doc ships before C4. Either the grub hole example must go, or radial holes in revolved parts need a dedicated construction (e.g., subtract from the *unrolled* θ-z wall parameterization before stitching), or forcing-op (1) moves into scope.
- Competing systems treat off-axis subtraction as table stakes, not an advanced feature: https://github.com/BelfrySCAD/BOSL2/wiki/Tutorial-Attachment-Attach — `diff() cube(50) attach(RIGHT, TOP, inside=true, shiftout=0.01) cylinder(...)` is the *tutorial* idiom, on any face at any angle; every B-rep CAD (Onshape/Fusion/SolidWorks hole features on arbitrary faces/planes) likewise. Users coming from any of these will attempt a side hole in week one — the orb lamp itself plausibly wants a lateral cord exit or set-screw, which is the same op.
- The z-band model is still a good default (countersinks/counterbores as clean 2D booleans is genuinely robust), but the doc should state the expressiveness boundary explicitly and route `radial`/angled holes to a named mechanism instead of exhibiting one in a sketch the model can't produce.

### 6. Lamp safety

- Supporting the doc's stance: https://3dwithus.com/3d-printed-lamp-shades-ideas-led-vs-incandescent-bulbs — empirical test: incandescent deformed/discolored a PLA shade in 2–3 h; LED stayed safe; keep ≥30 mm bulb-to-plastic and never fully enclose the bulb. LED-only is well grounded. UL 496 (https://ulnormdoc.com/wp-content/preview/UL%20496%202022.pdf) is the lampholder safety standard for the US/Canada; a printed thread carrying mains could never meet it, so "holder around a purchased socket, never printed mains threads" is the correct and conservative division — no source was found arguing a *passive* printed holder around a listed socket is per-se unsafe, but note printed PLA/PETG has no UL 94 flammability rating, and luminaire listings (UL 1598, per https://forums.mikeholt.com/threads/lampholders.32148/post-507026 — (unverified snippet)) monitor temperature on adjacent combustible materials for a reason: a homemade fixture is unlisted regardless.
- Contesting the thermal margin of "PETG near socket": https://www.electronicshub.org/led-bulbs-get-hot/ — LED bulb heatsinks run 60–100 °C depending on model and airflow, and sealed compact bulbs' internal driver electronics reach ~86 °C; the hottest zone is the *base/neck* — exactly where a collar gripping the socket sits, inside an orb with restricted convection. Meanwhile PETG's continuous-use ceiling is ~70 °C with HDT 65–75 °C and Tg ~80 °C (https://www.wevolver.com/article/petg-temperature-resistance-heat-limits-and-practical-insights-for-engineers, https://www.3dsourced.com/guides/petg-glass-transition-temperature/). "PETG near socket" is directionally right (better than PLA's ~60 °C Tg) but the margin can be zero or negative for a compact high-wattage LED in an enclosed pendant. Also note IEC 60238 lampholders carry T-ratings (e.g., T250) precisely because socket zones get hot (https://standards.globalspec.com/std/14207721/iec-60238). The doc should add a wattage cap, a clearance/ventilation rule around the bulb base/heatsink, and (given the tile survey's own creep findings — PETG creeps below 60 °C under sustained load) a note that the collar must not rely on sustained elastic grip near the socket.

## Recommended doc changes

1. **Qualify the novelty claim (§1 goal 2 and survey §(c) runner-up).** Replace "No surveyed system ships this (§3) — it is the deliberately novel part" with:

   > Dimensional fit contracts are not new in themselves — ISO 286 limits-and-fits is a century-old contract language; SolidWorks' Hole Wizard and dimension tolerancing carry H7/g6-style fit classes on features and TolAnalyst validates stack-ups across assemblies; and BOSL2's own screws.scad ships thread tolerance classes ("6g"/"2A"), named clearance-hole fits ("close"/"normal"/"loose"), and a separate `$slop` printer-compensation knob. What no surveyed system does is attach the contract to the *general mating interface itself* and make the *connect operation* fail compilation on kind/dimension/fit mismatch: BOSL2's `attach()` aligns frames but knows nothing about the screw library's tolerances two files away; commercial mate connectors are frames without dimensions. Bikar's novelty is that narrower composition — contract-on-port plus connect-time validation for arbitrary printed interfaces — not the existence of fit semantics.

   Also delete or rewrite §5's "separates two things every surveyed ecosystem conflates" — BOSL2 screws.scad separates exactly these two things (tolerance class vs `$slop`); say instead "generalizes a separation that today exists only inside thread/screw generators to every port kind."

2. **Rewrite the §8 rationale for the no-CSG core (divergence justification, ready to paste):**

   > The mainstream position is now the opposite of ours: OpenSCAD made Manifold its default backend in August 2025 "after a long time of battle testing" (openscad mailing list), booleans there are 5–30× faster than fast-csg (openscad/openscad#4533), and Manifold's output is guaranteed manifold by construction — so "avoid 3D CSG for robustness/performance" is no longer a defensible reason, and we do not claim it. We keep the constructive core for two reasons that survive Manifold's maturity: the print-validation gate needs a robust per-layer 2D region algebra regardless, so 2D-first geometry is paid for twice over; and stitched meshes retain semantic labels (which face belongs to which strut, band, or port) that a boolean result erases and our contract/gate tooling depends on. Manifold's own docs concede sub-ε sliver triangles can survive and error compounds across chained ops — acceptable for rendering, unhelpful for a pipeline whose value proposition is meshes that pass gates by construction.

3. **Fix the fit-ladder framing (§4.3/§5), paste-ready:**

   > These defaults transcribe one calibrated-printer source (Creative3DP's press-fit ladder) and assume hole compensation is applied separately by the printer profile. Mainstream guidance quotes much larger raw clearances — Hubs recommends 0.5 mm nominal for FDM snap-fits, and general tolerance guides give 0.4–0.6 mm for uncompensated FDM mating parts — precisely because they fold hole undersize, XY error, and material variation into one number. Our small intent gaps are only valid downstream of a measured printer profile; the C2 fit coupon is therefore not optional polish but the mechanism that makes this table honest. Note also the sign convention: our "press −0.10" is designed *interference*; several guides' "press fit 0.1 mm" is per-side *clearance*.

4. **Resolve the grub-hole contradiction (§4.1 vs §4.2/§8).** Either delete `hole grub radial 3.2` from the BulbCollar sketch, or add to §4.2: "Radial holes through revolved walls are *not* z-band holes — they are §8 forcing-op (1) (a hole through a curved shell) and are rejected until C4, or implemented as a dedicated θ-z wall-parameterization subtraction if C3 needs the set-screw."
