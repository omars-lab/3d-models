## Counter-evidence deep dives

### 1. Covestro/Bayer snap-fit constants — attack failed; the PDF checks out completely

Fetched the 4.2 MB PDF, extracted text, and rasterized the pages carrying image-only content. Verified directly:

- **Table 1 (p.9, read from image)**: y = 0.67·ε·l²/h (design 1, rectangle), y = 1.09·ε·l²/h (design 2, taper to h/2), y = 0.86·ε·l²/h (design 3, width to b/4), P = (bh²/6)·(E_s·ε/l).
- **">60%"**: "This version increases the permissible deflection by more than 60% compared to a snap-fitting arm of constant cross section (design 1)." Worked example I uses 1.09 numerically.
- **Strain rules**: "partially crystalline materials may be stressed almost to the yield point, amorphous ones up to about 70% of the yield strain… [glass-filled] about half the elongation at break"; "…rejoining, use about 60% of these values" (used numerically in example III: 0.6 × 2.5%).
- **"Stress-free"**: "After the joining operation, the snap-fit features should return to a stress-free condition." (Doc says "essentially stress-free" — a softening the parent doc's B.2 already justifies.)
- **W formula + friction**: W = P(μ+tanα)/(1−μtanα); Table 3 PC 0.45–0.55 (×1.2 same-plastic); example uses μ = 0.50×1.2.
- **R/h**: Fig 9 text confirms the 0.6 flattening AND the caution that R/h=0.6 creates thick sections/sinks; "the radius should not be less than 0.015 in. in any instance."

Cross-lineage spread (worth one caveat line in the doc): the Machine Design/AlliedSignal and Ticona-lineage treatments allow only **50% of yield strain for amorphous resins** where Covestro allows 70%, and add a Q base-rotation factor Covestro omits ([Machine Design](https://www.machinedesign.com/archive/article/21818595/new-equations-make-fastening-plastic-components-a-snap), [gotstogo Ticona-lineage PDF (unverified snippet)](https://www.gotstogo.com/misc/engineering_info/Snap_Fitsres72dpi.PDF)). Covestro is the *liberal* end of the injection-molding doctrine; the doc's other safety factors absorb this.

### 2. The ~2% PETG strain budget — the chain is broken in the middle but the endpoint holds

- **Factor 1 (7–10% PETG vendor band): UNGROUNDED as cited.** Two independent fetches of [the cited Unionfab article](https://www.unionfab.com/blog/2025/06/3d-print-snap-fit) — one asking for the specific claims, one demanding *every* number on the page — found **no strain percentages at all** (its numbers: 0.2–0.5 mm clearance, 2–3× arm length, 30–45° hooks, 0.5–1.0× fillets…). No other source giving "7–10% for PETG" was found. The Fictiv/Core77 strain table gives PLA 4–8%, ABS 7%, nylon 4–15% — no PETG row.
- **Factor 2 (0.6 rejoin): GROUNDED** (Covestro verbatim; independently the Ticona lineage uses the same 60%-for-reuse rule).
- **Factor 3 (½ for cross-layer flexure): GROUNDED but mis-cited.** The real source is [Core77/Fictiv, verbatim](https://www.core77.com/posts/65318/how-to-design-snap-fit-components): "If your cantilever can only be created in the Z axis, be aware elongation at break is reduced by 50% and tensile strength by 20-30%… We recommend reducing the allowable stress/strain by 50% for Z axis cantilevers." So the halving is documented practice, not made up — but it is Fictiv doctrine, not Unionfab's.
- **Steelman ("printed PETG snaps run 4–5% and survive")**: no source states this outright; what exists is the observation that community twist-locks (SnapLock etc.) ship with no strain analysis and work. Conversely [Hackaday's independent heuristic](https://hackaday.com/2022/11/11/oh-snap-3d-printing-snapping-parts-without-breakage/) — "use 10 or 15% of the [elongation-at-break] value to assure the part doesn't lose its shape" — gives 2.4–3.6% for PETG's 24%, straddling the doc's ~2%. A Covestro-style chain from PETG's ~5–6% yield-region strain (amorphous → 70% → ×0.6) also lands at ~2.1–2.5%. The ~2% endpoint is defensible from three chains; only the doc's specific derivation path is broken.

### 3. Detent 0.3–0.5 mm — both attributions verified verbatim

- [Firgelli](https://www.firgelliauto.com/blogs/mechanisms/bayonet-joint): "Typically 0.3-0.5 mm of axial relief gives a clear tactile click without locking up so hard that the user can't release it." Also verified: 90° default, 60° BNC, pins ⌀1.5–3 mm (6 heavy), 0.1–0.2 mm clearance, 5–20 N seating force. **One drift**: the page as fetched says slot corner radius "at least 0.5 mm"; survey/doc say "0.3–0.5 mm".
- [SnapLock README](https://github.com/flight505/SnapLock): "Minimal column protrusion (0.3–0.5 mm) + tip fillet", ⌀0.8 columns / ⌀1.2 notches, "Entry clearance: 5° (25° entry − 20° slot)", 45° self-supporting tab chamfer, print-orientation guidance — all verified. (SnapLock states **no material recommendation** — the survey never claimed it did; fine.)
- Spread: a popular [MakerWorld parametric twist-lock coupling uses ~0.2 mm of lock depth (unverified snippet)](https://makerworld.com/en/models/1376395-parametric-twist-and-lock-coupling) — the low end of the range is soft. The doc already handles this correctly by making every detent dimension a `param` decided by coupon W-C1 Q2.

### 4. ~30° twist — precedent verified, but retention-vs-angle is simply unstudied

SnapLock's 20°/4-tab lock is real. But: (a) [Seetronic](https://seetronic.com/blog/what-is-a-bayonet-connector-a-guide-to-quick-disconnect-solutions/) puts the *hardware* norm at "twist about 60–120 degrees", wider than the doc's "90° norm" framing; (b) Firgelli offers **no guidance below 60°** and nothing on retention adequacy vs angle; (c) SnapLock is an enclosure lid — a lower-stakes retention job than holding four wall tiles coplanar. No source argues 30° is inadequate; equally, no source affirms it. This is a genuinely coupon-decided bet and the doc should say so slightly more loudly (it nearly does, via Q2).

### 5. Keyhole dimensions — correct for *slotted* pan heads; Phillips pan is 0.5 mm taller

- #8 slotted pan ⌀0.322 in / H 0.096 in confirmed against a [B18.6.3 mirror table](https://torqbolt.com/asme-b18-6-3-slotted-pan-head-machine-screws-dimensions-standards-specifications) (Engineers Edge itself now 403s). #8 major ⌀ = 0.164 in = 4.17 mm (definitional). Flat-head table [confirmed at GlobalFastener](https://www.globalfastener.com/standards/detail_4972.html) (#8: 0.312/0.285, 0.100 ref).
- **Refinement found**: [B18.6.3 Type-2 cross-recessed (Phillips) pan heads](https://boltingspecialist.com/dimensions/asme-b18-6-3-type-2-cross-recessed-pan-head-machine-screws/index.html) run taller — **#8 max head height 0.115 in = 2.92 mm** (vs slotted 2.44). Phillips is what a user will actually buy. The doc's cavity (design head 3.1 mm, cavity 3.6 mm) still clears 2.92 mm, but its §3 sentence "#8 pan head ⌀8.18 × 2.44" is not the worst-case pan head.
- ⌀9.5/3/8-in norm: [Craftparts plate confirmed 23/64 in, #6 flathead, 30 lb](https://www.craftparts.com/products/1-3-4-keyhole-mounting-plate); Rockler bit page now 406 — mark (unverified snippet).

### 6. Bridging — the 10 mm rule is real but is the floor of a wide spread; ⌀10.5 is not actually "at the edge" of modern capability

- Verified verbatim: [Hydra "Horizontal bridges without support should not be longer than 10 mm"](https://www.hydraresearch3d.com/design-rules); [rahix "The roof sits 0.4 mm above the theoretical circle so slight drooping of the bridge won't affect the fit"](https://blog.rahix.de/design-for-3d-printing/) plus teardrop-for-small/flat-roof-for-large and "bridges on top of bridges".
- Counter-spread: [UltiMaker ~10 mm standard / 25 mm Tough PLA (page 403 — unverified snippet)](https://ultimaker.com/learn/design-for-fff-3d-printing-maximize-your-success/); [Creality: 20–25 mm clean on well-tuned printers (unverified snippet)](https://www.creality.com/blog/3d-printing-bridging); [Ucuz3D: 50–80 mm on optimized setups (unverified snippet)](https://www.ucuz3d.com/en/maximum-span-in-bridge-design-how-far-can-you/); and decisively in-domain, **Multiboard's own snap listings require printers to "bridge gaps of up to 30 mm"** ([Thangs listing, 403 — unverified snippet](https://thangs.com/designer/Multiboard/3d-model/Multiboard%20Snap-1311031)) — the exact printer class W2 targets routinely prints 3× the doc's worry span in production wall systems.
- Two survey attribution wrinkles: rahix does NOT give a numeric bridge span, a "stepped roof", or the sequential-bridging staged protocol — the staging technique is the [Hackaday bores piece](https://hackaday.com/2023/05/28/3d-printing-bores-without-support/) (unfetched, plausible), and "conservative ~10 mm" is Hydra alone.

### 7. The warp non-number — the novelty claim survives, but three citations around it are shaky

Tried hard to refute "no published quantitative warp figure for ~100×100×4 mm PETG on textured PEI":

- [Alsoufi & Elsayed (IJMME-IJENS 17:04, 2017)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318654219_Warping_Deformation_of_Desktop_3D_Printed_Parts_Manufactured_by_Open_Source_Fused_Deposition_Modeling_FDM_System): per-corner digital height-gauge protocol confirmed; results reported as **percent error** (2.0% corner, <1% best case) on PLA+ — not PETG, not mm-band-for-plate. (unverified beyond search summaries)
- [Techscience CMC 68(3)](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v68n3/42468/html) (fetched): macro **ABS** part, warp 3.7 mm → 0.8 mm after tuning — quantitative warp literature exists, wrong material.
- [De Gruyter, Int. Polymer Processing 2025, "Evaluation of print surface adhesion in FFF/FDM"](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ipp-2025-0113/html) (unverified snippet): PLA/**PETG**/ASA on six surfaces including PEI film and a textured plate — adhesion-focused, no plate-warp mm figure surfaced. This is the nearest miss and should be cited as such.
- [Armillotta et al., "Warpage of FDM parts: Experimental tests and analytic model"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320103445_Warpage_of_FDM_parts_Experimental_tests_and_analytic_model) (unverified snippet): the standard analytic warp model — ABS.

So: qualified as "none of the surveyed literature/community sources", the claim stands. **However**: (a) the **1–3 mm band's real home is [WhyItFailed](https://whyitfailed.fyi/blog/petg-warping-causes-and-how-to-fix-it)** ("bowed upward by 1–3 mm but still firmly attached") — the cited [Prusa forum thread contains no numbers at all](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/original-prusa-i3-mk3s-mk3-how-do-i-print-this-printing-help/large-printed-parts-are-not-flat-even-though-they-dont-unstick-from-bed/) (fetched: purely qualitative); (b) the [Bambu forum post /3](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/petg-warping-on-a-new-p1s/85455/3) as fetched is the OP's *problem report*, not the "bed 80–85 °C, ~8 mm brim, aux fan off" advice; (c) the practice bundle is contested anyway — WhyItFailed: "A 5 mm brim is plenty. Going to 10–15 mm just adds material", part fan 30–40% rather than off, bed 80 °C ✓; [Prusa PETG guide](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/petg_2059): bed 85/90 °C ✓ but flatly states PETG "does not shrink or warp (it has very little thermal expansion)" — directly opposing WhyItFailed's "PETG contracts 0.5–0.7%, more than PLA" which the survey leans on.

### 8. PLA-clips-are-an-error — mainstream guidance sides with the doc; the steelman is real but bounded

- With the doc: [Wevolver](https://www.wevolver.com/article/how-to-3d-print-snap-fit-joints) "PLA is not a suitable material… can crack or snap on their first use"; [Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing/) prefers ABS/PETG/nylon, calls PLA brittle.
- Against: [Fictiv's table lists PLA at 4–8% acceptable strain](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components) — i.e., a design-allowables source that treats PLA snaps as designable; [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2022/03/14/3d-printing-snap-fit-joints/) is only mildly negative ("might not be the best choice") with commenters reporting working PLA snaps; **Tough PLA** is explicitly marketed for "living hinges and snap fits" ([TriMech/MakerBot, 403 — unverified snippet](https://store.trimech.com/blog/makerbot-tough-pla-living-hinges-and-snap-fit)); and the parent doc's own B.3 concedes a week-long PLA clip test survived. The strongest pro-error fact remains parent B.3's aging argument (ambient embrittlement → fracture on the engage flex), which none of the pro-PLA sources address.
- Verdict: error is defensible for a *generated* part where PETG is the zero-cost default, but the doc should acknowledge that `pla` is a materials-policy call (aging), not settled snap-fit doctrine, and note the annealed/tough-PLA escape hatch is already the parent doc's own error message.

### 9. Ecosystem attributions — three verbatim hits, two wobbles

- [openGrid board guide](https://www.opengrid.world/guides/board/): **both quotes verbatim** ("primarily for alignment rather than structural support, though multiple connected boards do provide mutual reinforcement"; "better print slightly smaller (like 99.5%)"). Note the 99.5% advice is on the **board** guide only — the [snaps guide](https://www.opengrid.world/guides/snaps/) (fetched) doesn't carry it, contrary to the survey's dual citation.
- [3DWithUs HSW](https://3dwithus.com/honeycomb-storage-wall-guide-3d-printed-tool-organization): "Print one test insert first. Before printing 30 inserts, print one and check the fit." verbatim; "mount at least three corners" ✓ (M3 linkage looser than survey implies).
- Multiboard 0.25 mm: [core-parts docs page](https://docs.multibuild.io/beginner-section/core-parts-documentation) (fetched) does **not** contain it and doesn't mention quad snaps; the tolerance line lives on the Thangs listings (403 today; search snippets across multiple listings consistently read "designed with a tolerance of 0.25 mm"). Downgrade to (unverified snippet) or cite a listing that fetches.
- [Gridfinity spec](https://gridfinity.xyz/specification/) (fetched): magnets 6×2 + M3 corners are **block-to-baseplate**, not "baseplates to the surface" as the survey has it; the load-bearing part of the claim — no native plate-to-plate joining — is confirmed.

### 10. Canvas-2D over three.js — right call, unsourced threshold

- The engine fact (Lab is Canvas-2D) is repo ground truth; the survey's [InstancedMesh docs citations](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html) are live and accurate (minor wording drift: docs now read "A special version of a mesh…", "Use this class if…"). [Issue #30352](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/issues/30352) verified via `gh`: "InstancedMesh significantly slower than Mesh with shared attributes" — few-instance/heavy-geometry regressions are real, which *supports* not adopting instancing at N=4.
- The "~50" figure appears nowhere outside the survey's own analysis, and its one cited performance article ([threejsroadmap](https://threejsroadmap.com/blog/draw-calls-the-silent-killer)) is now 403 and was never quoted with numbers. Steelman for adopting three.js now (future 3D wall/orb previews, picking, lighting) is genuinely weakened by the repo fact that orbs already render in the Canvas-2D Lab — there is no separate three.js future the wall preview would be pre-investing in. The bet stands; the threshold number should be de-numbered or labeled as internal judgment.

## Recommended doc changes

**A. §3 strain-budget bullet — replace derivation citation (required fix).** Suggested replacement text:

> The working strain budget for printed PETG is ~2% effective. Three independent chains land there: (1) Covestro's amorphous-resin rule (≈70% of yield-region strain, PETG yield ~5–6%) times the 0.6 factor for repeatedly-joined parts gives ~2.1–2.5%; (2) the community heuristic of designing to 10–15% of elongation-at-break (Hackaday) gives 2.4–3.6% of PETG's ~24%; (3) Fictiv/Core77's FDM anisotropy rule — "elongation at break is reduced by 50% [in Z] … we recommend reducing the allowable stress/strain by 50% for Z axis cantilevers" — which we sidestep entirely by keeping every flexing member in the XY plane, holding it in reserve as margin. (A previously-cited "7–10% PETG vendor design band" could not be re-verified at its source and is withdrawn.)

**B. §4.3 keyhole — pan-head qualification (required fix).** Add after the screw table:

> Head dimensions above are ASME B18.6.3 *slotted* pan values (#8: ⌀8.18 × 2.44 mm); the more common cross-recessed (Phillips) pan head runs taller — #8 max 0.115 in = 2.92 mm (B18.6.3 Type 2) — which the 3.1 mm design head height and 3.6 mm cavity still clear. Flat-head (countersunk) screws are out of scope for the keyhole cavity.

**C. Q4 / §4.3 bridging — divergence justification (paste-ready).**

> Divergence note — bridge conservatism: credible counter-evidence says a ⌀10.5 mm bridge is nowhere near modern limits: Multiboard's official snaps require printers to "bridge gaps of up to 30 mm", community guidance puts clean unsupported bridges at 20–25 mm on well-tuned machines, and UltiMaker documents 25 mm with Tough PLA. We keep Hydra's ≤10 mm rule anyway because the cavity roof is a cosmetic-and-functional seat printed at production settings without per-part tuning, and the flat-roof + 0.4 mm droop allowance (rahix) costs nothing. Q4's "at the edge of the rule" means the edge of *our conservative rule*, not the edge of printability.

**D. §4.2 PLA error — divergence justification (paste-ready).**

> Divergence note — `material pla` as ERROR: the counter-position is real. Fictiv's snap-fit allowables table designs PLA to 4–8% strain, hobbyist reports of long-lived PLA clips exist, and toughened PLA is marketed specifically for snap fits and living hinges (MakerBot/TriMech). We keep the hard error because the clip is a *generated* part where PETG costs the user one spool swap, and the parent doc's B.3 hazard — ambient physical aging collapsing PLA ductility so an aged arm fractures on the engage/disengage flex — is exactly the load case a wall clip sees years after printing, and none of the pro-PLA sources address aging. If a tough/annealed-PLA story is wanted, that is a new material word with its own datasheet, not a bypass of this error.

**E. §3 twist-angle bullet — qualification.** Change "below the 90° hardware norm" to "below the 60–120° hardware norm (Firgelli's 90° default; Seetronic's 60–120° range)" and add: "no surveyed source quantifies retention as a function of twist angle below 60° — the 30°/four-arm geometry rests on the SnapLock 20°/four-tab existence proof and is confirmed or rejected by coupon W-C1 Q3, not by literature."
