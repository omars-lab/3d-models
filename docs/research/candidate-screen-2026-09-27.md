# Candidate screen, consolidated, 2026-09-27

This is the file to act on. Two researchers screened new construction candidates from
creators other than Sarah Brewer, each without reading the other. This file puts their
verdicts side by side, settles where they disagree, re-opens the sources behind every
GO, and ends with one queue in rebuild order. The two screens stay as the record it was
built from.

## How the check was done

- **No video was downloaded.** For each re-checked video I read `yt-dlp --skip-download`
  metadata (title, channel, upload date, length, chapters, description, licence field),
  the thumbnail, and for most GOs one or two storyboard sprite sheets. A storyboard sheet
  is 25 small preview frames, about one every 1–10 s depending on the video's length.
  Frames are small, so a legibility call from them is a judgment, not a viewing.
- **GeoGebra:** the page (WebFetch), the materials API record, and the preview image the
  API links.
- **Marks used below.** *Re-verified* = I opened the source and it says what the claim
  says. *Snippet-only* = the claim rests on a search-result line nobody fetched.
  *Could not check* = I tried and could not settle it, or did not try; the reason is given.
- **Coaster fit is still a guess.** Neither researcher watched a video end to end, and
  neither did I. Every coaster-fit call here is judged from thumbnails and storyboard
  frames. The ledger has shown twice (bknVRSMcLj0, n3IidKfXE1I) that a pattern that looks
  open in a video can read as near-solid at 90 mm. Each GO still needs the normal render
  and look before anything is printed.

## The four disagreements

### n_ICgwOr6qs, Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque fivefold motif: GO (A's side)

- **B's evidence is real but covers only the second finish.** The chapters (yt-dlp,
  re-verified) are: Introduction 0:00, *Constructing The Pattern* 1:20, Painting I –
  Outlining 8:06, Painting I – Painting 14:48, Painting II – Tracing & Weaving 18:45,
  Painting II – Transferring & Painting 24:49, *Painting II – Outlining freehand curves*
  27:32. The title card says "Two Ideas for the … Fivefold Motif".
- **The construction itself is compass work.** The storyboard sheet covering about
  4:08–8:16 (re-verified) shows every step made with a compass: overlapping circles and
  arcs building a five-petal flower, then the finished Painting I piece. The sheet for
  8:16–12:24 shows Painting I's outline also drawn with the compass. Freehand curves
  appear only in the last chapter of the second, optional finish.
- **Call:** GO, rebuilding the 1:20–8:06 construction and the Painting I outline only;
  Painting II is out of scope. A's two hedges stand: it is built from arcs, so it likely
  needs the arc path nmEjCTzMbDg used, and the woven band may render denser than it looks.
  That the motif is 5-fold rests on the title and series (#fivefoldfun); I did not count
  the petals in a frame with certainty at storyboard size.

### _U6G8QSfWnk, Umm Al Girih tutorial 1: GO, conditional (both sides partly right)

- **B is right that it is a girih tiling.** Chapters (re-verified): "Introduction to the
  5 girih shapes" 0:13, "Drawing the Fivefold Rectangle" 8:05, "Drawing the Proportioning

## Licence position

- **YouTube.** yt-dlp's `license` field came back empty for all 12 videos I re-read (every video
  GO or disputed in either screen, plus vpB4VAqOduo); that field is filled only when the watch page shows
  a Creative Commons marker. A also checked the marker against a known CC video, one
  positive control. So each video is **presumed**, not confirmed, to carry the standard
  YouTube licence: we may watch it and rebuild the construction from it, but not download
  or republish the video or its frames. No creator page either researcher fetched states a
  licence.
- **Rebuilding versus copying.** Both researchers hedge the same way: a geometric
  construction method is generally not what copyright protects, but a book's text and
  diagrams are, and the historical patterns themselves (Mamluk, Mughal, Timurid, Bourgoin
  1879) are centuries old. That is the researchers' reading, not legal advice. Credit the
  creator and any named source in the ledger row: Broug's book for A9fefFurD_s,
  vpB4VAqOduo and cjAFU4ck; Aljanabi and Sarvdalir for 0ke_GpoBa-s's methods; Bourgoin
  plate 170 for jlTmt_279M4; Mian's gBV_JTt3Kxk for yZN_wn0uvTY.
- **GeoGebra.** The terms (re-verified) say: "We grant you permission to use the Website
  Content under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution-Non-Commercial-ShareAlike
  license (version 4.0 or later)" and "You must not use any part of the Website Content
  for any commercial purpose without obtaining a License to do so from us." A reads this
  as covering user-uploaded materials; B's hedge stands: the text does not say in so many
  words that "Website Content" includes other users' uploads, so read it as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
  *probably*, and record the author and material id either way.
- **If coasters are sold.** A rebuild from a video is the route every Brewer coaster took.
  A rebuild that copies a GeoGebra file (hzyhmg9p, cjAFU4ck) likely falls under NC, so it
  could not be sold without a licence from GeoGebra, and a construction lifted from Broug's
  book adds his copyright on top. Bourgoin (1879) is on archive.org under the Public Domain
  Mark (B fetched it), so jlTmt_279M4's pattern can be checked against the plate without
  that restriction.
