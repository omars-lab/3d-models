# Coaster market prices: what the market band looks like (researcher A)

The question: what do decorative coasters sell for, in what set sizes, with what breaks for
bulk, and what do buyers pay extra for — so a cost-plus price for a ~110 mm, 4–5 mm, one- or
two-color PLA Islamic geometric coaster can be shown beside a market band.

Every number here comes from `docs/research/coaster-market-pricing-a-raw.md`, where each
listing has its URL and whether I opened the page (**fetched**) or only saw a search snippet
(**snippet**). Row numbers below (r45 etc.) point into that file.

**Two limits on everything below.** First, these are **listed (asking) prices, not sold
prices** — nothing I reached shows what a buyer paid. Second, Etsy, eBay and Amazon refused
automated fetches and the browser extension was not connected, so most 3D-printed competitor
numbers are snippets. A snippet can be stale or pair a price with the wrong item; two of mine
disagreed with the fetched page (Chemist Tree: snippet $26, page $35; Leighton House: snippet
£19, page £24), both times the snippet lower.

---

## 1. Where a ~110 mm 3D-printed geometric coaster sits

### Against other 3D-printed coasters (the direct band)

Over **21** physical 3D-printed coaster listings with a stated set size (4 fetched, 17 snippet;
raw Q4c), using the shown price divided per coaster:

| Measure | Per coaster | As a set of 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Low | $2.50 | $10 |
| Lower quarter | about $3.30 | about $13 |
| **Median** | **$3.88** | **about $15.50** |
| Upper quarter | about $6.20 | about $25 |
| High | $8.75 | $35 |

The 4 fetched listings alone span the same band: $3.33 (r47, set of 6 + holder, $20), $3.75
(r46, PLA set of 4, $14.99), $5.24 (r45, two-color PLA set of 4 + holder, $20.97), $8.75 (r48,
PETG + cork set of 4, $35).

**Confidence: medium on the band, low on the exact median.** 21 listings is a fair spread, but
17 are snippets and none are sold prices.

What puts a listing at the top of the band, from the listings themselves: a premium material or
finish (PETG with cork, r48), a seller showing a markdown from a higher "original" (r53, $26.39
from $32.99), or a theme (the Islamic set of 5 at $35, r40). The bottom of the band is licensed
or novelty prints and one-color sets with no holder.

### Against Islamic / Moroccan-pattern 3D-printed coasters (the closest competitors)

**4 listings, all Etsy snippets** (r40–r43): $35.00 (set of 5, Arabic calligraphy), $23.26
(Moroccan pattern, set size not stated, possibly 6), $25.00 (Kufic calligraphy, set size not
stated), $18.99 (Arabic coffee set, set size not stated). A fifth (r44, $7.00 for "4") is low
enough to be a file or a single and is left out.

So Islamic-themed 3D-printed **sets list at about $19–$35**, the upper half of the generic band
or above it. **Confidence: low** — 4 snippets, and only one has a known set size ($7.00 per
coaster for the set of 5). None of the 5 could be opened, and none of the searches I ran turned
up an Islamic-pattern 3D-printed seller with its own shop site.

### Against Islamic geometric coasters in other materials (the ceiling)

| Listing | Material | Set of 4 | Seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leighton House museum shop, 110 mm square, Islamic-art inspired (r1) — **the closest size and design match found** | laser-cut MDF, cork | £24.00 (about $31 at an assumed ~$1.30 per £; rate not fetched) | fetched |
| Zahra, Islamic geometric brass inlay (r3) | marble | $44.99 (from $49.99), gift box | fetched |
| Aga Khan Museum, 10-pointed star (r2) | maple, boxed | $145.00 | fetched |
| Redbubble Islamic / Arabic geometric (r10, r11) | printed MDF | $15.41, $18.13 | snippet |
| MadeMe Moroccan-style (r9) | decoupaged ceramic | £14.50 | fetched |

A museum shop prices a laser-cut MDF set of the same size at about twice the 3D-printed
median. That is a ceiling a design-led maker can point to, not a band it can assume: the museum
sells provenance and footfall as well as the coaster.

### Size is a transfer condition the market does not price

Most 3D-printed competitors are 3.5–4 in (about 89–100 mm); the MAD Made coaster (r46) is about
120 mm and is in the lower half of the band. None of the 21 listings shows a higher price for a
larger coaster. A 110 mm, 4–5 mm coaster uses more filament and printer time than a 90 mm,
3 mm one, so **cost** rises with size while the **market band** above does not visibly. The band
transfers to our coaster only as "what buyers see beside it", not as evidence that a larger
coaster earns more.

## 2. Set sizes to offer

From the set sizes stated across all physical listings in the raw file (raw Q2b):

| Set size | Listings seen | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **4** | about 40 | the default unit almost everywhere: museum shops, makers, Etsy, marble retailers |
| **6** | about 10 | second most common |
| single | 7 | mostly an option beside sets, or a premium piece |
| 5 | 2 sold | used for "one of each pattern" sets |
| 2 | 2 | rare |
| 8 | 3 | rare, at the low end per coaster ($2.62–$4.87) |

**Suggested offer: single, set of 4, set of 6**, with a "one of each design" set sized to the
number of designs when a collection has 5. **Confidence: medium** for 4 and 6 (counts are large
and agree with the one advisory source, Listybox, "sets of 4 or 6", which states no method);
**low** for single and 8 (few listings).

## 3. How price per coaster falls with set size

Only one seller shows a clean same-product curve, and it is a 3D-printed PLA coaster
(**MAD Made, r46, fetched from the store's variant data**):

| Set | Price | Per coaster | vs single |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $4.99 | $4.99 | — |
| 2 | $7.99 | $4.00 | −20% |
| 4 | $14.99 | $3.75 | −25% |

One more pair, weaker: Zsuzsanna Horvath plywood, set of 4 DKK 240 vs set of 6 DKK 295, **−18%
per coaster from 4 to 6** (fetched; but the 4 is painted and the 6 natural, so some of the gap
may be the paint). Heart of Anatolia's ceramic 4s ($3.68–$4.90 each) and 6s ($3.25–$5.80 each,
on sale) overlap and show no clear step.

Across sellers the picture is the same direction and noisier: 3D-printed singles list at $3.75,
$12 and $20 (3 Etsy snippets), well above the $3.88 per-coaster median for sets.

**Price breaks to model: a set of 4 at about 20–25% less per coaster than a single; a set of 6
at roughly another 15–20% less per coaster than the set of 4.** **Confidence: low** — the 1→4
step rests on 1 seller, the 4→6 step on 1 seller with a confound.

## 4. Bulk and wholesale breaks

**None of the 11 3D-printed shops and listings whose pages or snippets I read publishes a bulk
tier** (raw Q3). What other coaster sellers publish:

| Break | Source | Count | Seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% off at 3–5 units | glass coaster seller | 1 | snippet |
| −20% per coaster at 4 or more (from $12.50 to $10.00) | eBay cork personalized coasters | 1 | snippet |
| "Up to 20%" wholesale; bulk by custom quote | Contrado ceramic coasters | 1 | fetched |
| **Wholesale = 50% of retail ("keystone")**; first-order minimum $100–$150 suggested; wholesale and suggested retail must be no higher than on your other channels | Faire's brand guidance | 1 platform | snippet (Faire refused fetches) |
| Coaster brands on Faire set $100 and $200 order minimums at $18 and $34 suggested retail | 2 Faire listings | 2 | snippet |

**Price breaks to model:**

- **Small bulk (a few sets, a gift order): 10%**, the lower of the two published consumer breaks.
  Confidence: low (2 sources, both snippets).
- **Café / event order: up to 20%**, the most any published consumer or maker break went.
  Confidence: low (3 sources, 1 fetched).
- **Wholesale to a shop: 50% of retail**, if selling through stores or Faire. Confidence:
  medium (the platform's own stated convention, but a snippet). This one has a consequence for
  cost-plus: a wholesale line only exists if retail is at least twice cost, because the
  retailer takes the other half.

**Do not port the promotional-printing curves.** Paper, cork and foam promo coasters fall from
$1–$2 to under $1 each over thousands of units (raw Q3), because a setup fee is spread over the
run. A printed coaster's cost per piece is close to flat with quantity (each one is printer
time and filament), so that curve does not transfer; the breaks above are the ones made by
sellers whose cost behaves like ours.

## 5. What buyers pay extra for

| Extra | Finding | Count | Confidence | Model it as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Holder / stand | Bundled with most sets (13/13 Fantastic Plastics designs, "free holder"; most Etsy 3D-printed sets). Sold alone at $5.00 and $6.74 (from $8.99) | 2 standalone prices | medium that it is expected in a set; low on the standalone price | part of the set; a standalone holder at about $5–$9 |
| Gift box | Included in the price in every listing that mentions one; 0 of 5 charged separately | 5 | medium | a cost, not a revenue line |
| Custom color | Free in the 2 listings that offer it (Fantastic Plastics, 15+ colors; Etsy custom-color set) | 2 | low | no upcharge |
| Personalization | One priced upcharge: +$2.00 for a large letter (leather set). Personalized 3D-printed sets in the band sit at its low end ($11.99/4, $26.99/8) | 1 priced | low | a small fixed add-on at most; no evidence of a large premium |
| Islamic / Ramadan theme | Themed 3D-printed sets list at $19–$35 vs a ~$15.50 generic median per 4 | 4 snippets | low | a reason to sit in the upper half of the band, not a separate add-on |
| Museum provenance | Museum shops at about 2× (MDF, £24) to 5×+ (maple, $145) of maker prices for comparable sets | 2 | medium (both fetched) | a ceiling, not a band |

## 7. What would raise the confidence

- **Read Etsy directly in a browser** (the extension was not connected here): open rows 40–44
  for set sizes and current prices, and sort the 3D-printed coaster search by "top" to record
  20–50 listings with their review counts. Review counts are the nearest public stand-in for
  sales.
- **Sold prices**: eBay sold-listings filter (needs a browser) for "3D printed coasters".
- **One more same-seller curve** for 3D-printed sets of 4 vs 6, to check the 4→6 step.
- **Faire wholesale prices** need a retailer login; the 50% rule is the platform's stated
  convention, not a measured average.
