## Counter-evidence deep dives

### 1. The mechanical-clip bet ("do decorative tiles need interconnection at all?")

- https://www.art3d.com/ + https://www.amazon.com/Art3d-Panels-Living-Bedroom-Office/dp/B0F7R6SLVC — the largest commercial 3D-wall-panel ecosystem (PVC, plant fiber) is **glue-up, panel-by-panel, zero tile-to-tile connection**, sold in the millions of sq ft. This is direct existence proof that lightweight decorative relief tiles succeed with no interconnection.
- https://stickgoo.com/blogs/blogs/a-step-by-step-guide-on-how-to-install-3d-wall-panels — installation is adhesive-per-panel (PU/silicone S-pattern), roller to seat; a 2–3 mm edge expansion gap for PVC is mentioned in search results (unverified snippet for the exact figure's page).
- https://www.printables.com/model/206002-wallpanel-modular-hexagon-tiles — the research file's own flagship printed example: frames friction-fit to each other ("no glue needed when your printer is calibrated") but the *wall interface* is adhesive; direct fetch 404'd, existence and no-glue quote verified via search snippet.
- https://www.printables.com/model/854012-hexagon-wall-shelf-command-strip and https://www.printables.com/model/1055887-honeycomb-storage-wall-vertical-command-strip-hang — command strips are the norm in the printed-wall-decor community, including for load-bearing honeycomb walls (half-strips, vertical orientation).
- Steelman: for 30–80 g tiles, adhesive-per-tile is simpler, tolerates substrate waviness (each tile conforms), makes every tile independently replaceable, and eliminates the entire connector/creep/tolerance problem space. What clips *uniquely* buy is: coplanarity across joints on wavy drywall (rigid printed tiles do not conform like foam PVC), gap uniformity, and removable/re-hangable fields. That is an alignment argument, not a retention argument.
- **Should change the design**: connectors should be opt-in per wall (`connect none | clips | ...`), with adhesive-per-tile documented as the default for permanent interior installs. See draft below.

### 2. Zero-strain past-center detent clip (prior art + creep premise)

Prior art — this is a known fastener class, not a new mechanism:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzus_fastener and https://monroeaerospace.com/blog/what-are-quarter-turn-fasteners-and-how-do-they-work/ — quarter-turn fasteners (1930s): cam ramp draws the joint tight over ~90°, cross-pin drops into a detent at end of travel. **Note: the pin sits in the detent under maintained spring preload** — the classic detent class is not zero-strain; the spring is what makes it vibration-proof and rattle-free.
- https://southco.com/en_any_int/fasteners/emdzusemreg-quarter-turn-fasteners/emdzusemreg-turn-to-close-turn-to-open-quarter-turn-fasteners/d2-dzus-rapier-quarter-turn-fasteners — current commercial catalog of the same class.
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US4251134A/en (and US4017878A) — camera bayonet mounts: rotation + spring-loaded lock pin engaging a recess; retention is in shear through the bayonet lugs, with only a small anti-rattle spring stressed at rest. This is the closest prior art to the doc's "hold in shear/bearing, minimal rest strain" configuration.
- Internal contradiction found (no external source needed): §4 simultaneously claims the engaged clip is "unstressed" AND that capture depth ≥ 2× warp "so bowed tiles are pulled flush." Elastically flattening a 0.2–0.5 mm bowed tile stores energy; the flattening force must be reacted permanently by the jaws. Either the tile stays bowed (no sustained clip load) or it is held flat (sustained load somewhere, and PLA/PETG relaxation applies — possibly benignly, as the *tile* stress-relaxes flat over time). The doc should say "low-preload" and reason about where the flattening preload lives.

Creep premise counter-evidence:
- http://thrinter.com/creep-abs-pla-petg-alloy-910/ — empirical clip test: "the PLA version of the clip, even after a week under tension, still works as designed" despite permanent set. Directly softens "creeps loose within months" as a categorical.
- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/312872242_Long-Term_Creep_and_Impact_Strength_of_Biocompatible_3D-Printed_PLA-Based_Scaffolds — PLA/HA scaffolds under loads up to 10 MPa "did not change shape and lose mechanical strength" long-term; wall-tile clip body stresses (grams of tile across mm² of section) are orders of magnitude below typical creep-test stresses.
- https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/app.54463 — already in the research file; note it is a **stress relaxation** study, which is the correct phenomenon for a strain-controlled (deflected) clip — the file conflates creep and relaxation.
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0032386119310213 and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/macp.201900475 — PLA **physical aging**: stiffens and embrittles within days at ambient (ductility collapsing from ~250% to a few %). This is the better-grounded reason to ban PLA spring parts: an aged PLA arm fractures on engage/disengage flex. The failure mode is fracture-on-flex more than loosen-by-creep at these stresses.
- https://blog.uavmodel.com/pla-vs-petg-when-to-use-each-material-for-3d-printing-2026-guide/ — community consensus supporting the doc's direction (PLA loses clamp tension over days/weeks, prefer PETG for sustained tension) — so the PLA→PETG rule survives, on softer grounds.

### 3. Porting ceramic/grouted rules to printed plastic

- 3× rule: https://www.tileletter.com/about-grout-joint-width/ verified ANSI A108.02 §4.3.8 verbatim ("the joint width shall be three times the actual variation of the facial dimensions of the tile supplied"). The port (3× printer tolerance) is an analogy — ANSI's rule exists so *grout* can absorb facial variation; ungrouted printed gaps have no grout to protect, but the "gap must exceed accumulated dimensional error so tiles never touch" logic transfers acceptably.
- Nuance: TileLetter states 1/16" is ANSI's *minimum recommended* width and criticizes blanket 1/16" use — the doc's "never <1.6 mm" is a slight hardening of the source's framing.
- CTE arithmetic verified: PLA ≈ 68 ppm/°C (https://americanfilament.us/blogs/3d-printing-guide/pla-filament-properties, https://www.specialchem.com/plastics/guide/coefficient-of-linear-thermal-expansion); 100 mm × 20 K × 70e-6 = 0.14 mm ✓; 1 m × 20 K = 1.4 mm ✓. The doc's numbers are right.
- Materiality: for the doc's own stated interior case (heated living room, realistically ±5 K), the CTE term is 0.02–0.04 mm on a 100 mm tile — under 4% of the default 1.2 mm gap and smaller than FDM dimensional scatter. The term only becomes load-bearing in `sunlit`/`near_radiator` (ΔT 20–40 K). Commercial PVC 3D panels — with CTE similar to PLA — ship with only a 2–3 mm whole-wall edge gap and no per-joint movement budgeting (unverified snippet via https://stickgoo.com/blogs/blogs/a-step-by-step-guide-on-how-to-install-3d-wall-panels).
- Movement/run rule: https://www.garageflooringllc.com/expansion-and-contraction/ (verified) attributes tenting to *direct sun* on tightly interlocked floating floors and notes even a ½" perimeter gap "does not resolve the direct sunlight issue." https://www.constructionspecifier.com/specifying-movement-joints-and-sealants-for-tile-and-stone-reviewing-current-industry-standards-and-design-options/ (verified): EJ171 interior 20–25 ft, sun/moisture 8–12 ft. Both source regimes (grouted bonded ceramic; sun-blasted interlocked polypropylene) differ from an indoor decorative field with open 1.2 mm gaps and compliant clips: 10 open gaps provide ~12 mm of slack against ~0.1–1 mm of growth. The ~10-module rule cannot bite unless connectors are simultaneously rigid AND gap-filling — which the doc's own connector-placement rule already forbids in the field interior. It is a cheap, harmless validator but its physics justification indoors is circular.

### 4. Front-face registration / bow magnitude

- The 0.2–0.5 mm bow over 100 mm figure appears in the research file (§4, "typically") with **no citation**, and the design doc inherits it as fact plus derives the 1.0 mm capture default from it. No independent source states this band.
- What sources do say: large flat FDM parts are frequently not flat — https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/original-prusa-i3-mk3s-mk3-how-do-i-print-this-printing-help/large-printed-parts-are-not-flat-even-though-they-dont-unstick-from-bed/ (inverse/hill warping inherent to large flat PLA pieces); https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318654219_Warping_Deformation_of_Desktop_3D_Printed_Parts_Manufactured_by_Open_Source_Fused_Deposition_Modeling_FDM_System (quantified warp, %-scale corner error; mild corner lift is described as 1–3 mm in survey material). So the true range is roughly 0–3 mm depending on geometry/bed/material — the doc's band is a plausible midpoint but an invented one, and the capture-depth default keyed to it is similarly soft. The doc's own W2 clip-coupon calibration is the right fix; the static default should be labeled a placeholder.
- Leveling-clip analogy: DIYTileGuy (verified, partially) describes plate-under-tile + strap + cap/wedge pulling — the research file's stronger phrasing ("base plate sits under BOTH adjacent tiles", "bears on the TOP faces of both tiles simultaneously") is a fair mechanical reading of how these systems level across a joint but is **not verbatim in the cited source**, and the "tile cracking under clamped corners" item is posed in the article as an open concern, not a "reported failure mode." No source contradicts the analogy itself; it is reasonable design reasoning, resting on the doc's own synthesis.

### 5. Anchor-budget validator

- 30–50% tension derate confirmed at community/vendor grade: https://www.austingallery.org/blog/how-much-weight-can-drywall-hold, https://engineerfix.com/drywall-anchor-weight-chart-how-much-can-they-hold/, https://maden.co/blogs/fastening-joining/how-much-can-anchors-hold-in-drywall-load-ratings-guide (tensile ratings significantly below shear; package numbers are best-case).
- But the research file's own §5 scale check concedes the point: a full 3 m² field is 10–20 kg; a single toggle (75–150 lb, derated) carries the whole wall. A weight-vs-rating ERROR will essentially never fire on realistic specs. The real observed failure path in the survey is **adhesive strips** ("the most-reported adhesive failure path", 1–3 lb/strip) and temperature/humidity-sensitive adhesion ("command strips... WILL fall off eventually" — community comment surfaced in search, unverified snippet). No documented pull-out failures of screw anchors under printed-tile fields were found.
- **Should change the design**: keep the arithmetic (it is nearly free) but demote screw-anchor demand>supply to WARN and make the adhesive-strip budget the ERROR-grade check; add a humidity/temperature caveat for adhesive mounting.

### 6. Magnet pocket and keyhole dims

- Magnet-specific guidance contradicts the −0.1 mm interference default: https://kingroon.com/blogs/3d-printing-guides/how-to-embed-magnets-into-3d-prints (+0.2–0.4 mm clearance + glue); https://www.sovol3d.com/blogs/news/how-to-use-magnets-in-3d-printed-models (glue + chamfer guidance); https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/how-do-i-print-this-printing-help/how-to-safely-insert-magnets-into-a-print/ (6.15/3.1 mm pocket for a 6×3 magnet on one printer, too loose on another — printer-dependent). The −0.1 mm school exists (https://tools.creative3dp.com/blog/press-fit-tolerances-3d-printing/ fit ladder, press = −0.10 mm designed gap for rigid filaments), so this is a genuine disagreement between the general press-fit ladder and magnet-specific practice (brittle plated magnets chip; pockets vary). Verdict: −0.1 mm as an *auto* default is contested; clearance+glue is the safer default with press as opt-in intent.
- Keyhole: standard woodworking entry is 3/8" / 9.5 mm (https://www.rockler.com/hanging-slot-router-bits-router-bits, verified); the cited Printables 146312 itself says 8 mm entry / 4 mm slot is a *tight* fit for #8 — size up. Internal inconsistency in doc §6: "slot = shank + 0.5 mm" with #8 (thread OD ≈ 4.2 mm) yields ≈4.7 mm, but the worked example says "4 × 8 mm". The 4 mm slot matches a #6, not a #8. The idealinc.com citation returned 403 (unverified).

## Misgrounded or missing citations

1. **FDM bow "0.2–0.5 mm on 100 mm parts"** — stated as fact in research file §4 and inherited by doc §2/§4/§8; no citation exists anywhere. Literature range is ~0–3 mm depending on conditions. The 1.0 mm capture default and the "2× expected warp" check are keyed to an unsourced number.
2. **"Creeps loose in PLA within months"** — an extrapolation; no cited source gives a loosening timeline at wall-tile stress levels. The cited Wiley paper is a *stress relaxation* study (correct phenomenon, wrong label); Thrinter's empirical clip test points the other way at week scale.
3. **"PLA fares worst of common filaments for creep"** — contested; at room temperature the aging literature shows PLA *stiffens* (and embrittles). The stronger, well-sourced PLA hazard for clips is embrittlement-on-flex (Polymer/Macromol. Chem. Phys. aging papers), which the doc never cites.
4. **"PETG has ~30% stronger interlayer bonding than PLA"** (research file §7) — unverified and dubious as stated; PLA layer adhesion is commonly rated excellent. Not load-bearing for the doc; flag for removal or sourcing.
5. **Leveling-clip mechanism description** — over-precise vs. the cited DIYTileGuy article (see spot-check). The "front-face-to-front-face registration" framing is the survey's inference; fine as reasoning, wrong as attribution.
6. **Magnet −0.1 mm "standard recipe"** — the Printables 850069 citation was not verified; magnet-specific guides recommend the opposite (clearance + glue).
7. **Keyhole numbers** — internal inconsistency (4 mm slot vs shank+0.5 for #8); idealinc citation unfetchable.
8. **Missing prior-art citations for the corner clip** — Dzus/Southco quarter-turn fasteners and bayonet lens-mount patents are the direct ancestors of "rotate-to-detent, retain in shear" and should be cited both for credibility and for the design lesson that classic detents keep a small maintained preload for anti-rattle.
9. **Missing counter-position citation for the connector bet** — the commercial glue-up 3D-wall-panel market (Art3d et al.) is the largest real-world dataset on decorative relief tiles and appears nowhere in the survey.

## Recommended doc changes

### A. Make connectors opt-in; add divergence justification (paste-ready)

> **Why clips at all?** The strongest counter-position deserves stating: the dominant successful practice for lightweight decorative wall tiles — commercial PVC/plant-fiber 3D panels (Art3d) and nearly all printed hex-panel systems on Printables — uses **no tile-to-tile connection whatsoever**; each tile is glued or command-stripped to the wall independently, which tolerates wavy substrates, makes every tile replaceable, and deletes the entire connector/tolerance/creep problem space. We adopt that as the **default** (`connect none`). Clips are opt-in, and they are justified on *alignment*, not retention: rigid printed tiles (unlike foam PVC) do not conform to drywall waviness, so adjacent-tile coplanarity and gap uniformity in raking light are set by the substrate unless something registers the faces — that is the one job adhesive cannot do. `connect clips` is recommended only for removable installs, raking-light focal walls, and field-of-many-small-tiles cases where cumulative alignment drift is visible.

Corresponding grammar change: `wall` gains `connect none | clips | full` with `none` as default; the anchor/connector BOM and clip validators activate only when clips are on.

### B. Rename and re-derive the clip's "zero-strain" claim (paste-ready)

> **Prior art and an honest caveat.** The CornerClip is a member of a century-old fastener class — quarter-turn/Dzus fasteners (cam ramp + end-of-travel detent) and bayonet mounts (rotate, retain in shear via lugs, small spring only for anti-rattle). We cite them deliberately: the classic detent holds its pin *under maintained spring preload*, and a truly zero-preload bayonet rattles. Our claim is therefore **low-preload at rest**, not zero-strain: retention is in shear/bearing through the detent, but any tile bowed by 0.2–0.5 mm that the jaws pull flush stores elastic energy that the clip must react indefinitely. We accept this because (a) the flattening force for a 100 mm × 3–6 mm tile is small, (b) it is the *tile*, printed thick, that carries most of the stored strain, and its slow stress-relaxation flattens the tile rather than releasing the joint, and (c) the clip prints in PETG, whose relaxation at these stresses is modest. What we still rule out is retention that depends on a *thin spring arm's* maintained deflection.

### C. Soften and re-source the PLA creep rule (paste-ready)

> **PLA gate, restated.** The categorical "PLA clips creep loose in months" overstates the sourced evidence: an empirical week-long clip test (Thrinter) found a PLA clip under continuous tension still functional despite permanent set, and PLA scaffold studies show no shape change under sustained loads up to ~10 MPa — far above wall-tile clip stresses. The better-grounded PLA hazard is **physical aging**: PLA embrittles within days at ambient (ductility collapsing to a few percent), so an aged PLA spring arm is likely to *fracture during the engage/disengage flex*, and PLA additionally stress-relaxes faster than PETG under maintained deflection. The compile ERROR on `PLA + sustained-deflection` therefore stands, but its stated rationale becomes "embrittlement on flex + relaxation of preload," and the message should offer `PETG` (preferred) or annealed PLA, citing the aging literature rather than a loosening timeline we cannot source.

### D. Simplify the CTE term's role (paste-ready)

> **The CTE term is real physics but negligible indoors.** At PLA's ~68–80 ppm/K, a 100 mm tile in a heated living room (±5 °C) moves 0.02–0.04 mm — under 4% of the default 1.2 mm gap and smaller than FDM dimensional scatter; commercial PVC 3D panels (similar CTE) ship with only a 2–3 mm whole-wall edge gap and no per-joint movement budget. We keep the formula because it is free and because it *does* bite where the survey's failures actually happened — `sunlit` and `near_radiator` (ΔT 20–40 K), where the term reaches 0.15–0.3 mm/tile. Rule change: for `environment interior`, the CTE term is computed with ΔT_design = 5 K (not 15 K) and can never dominate; the 15–40 K ΔT values are activated by the environment attribute. Likewise the ~10-module rigid-run break is kept but honestly labeled: with open 1.2 mm gaps and compliant field joints it is unreachable by design; it exists as a guard for `dovetail` perimeter runs and future gap-filling connectors, not as core indoor physics.
