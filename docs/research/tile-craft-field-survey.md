# Field Research: Tiling Craft + Interlocking Panels → DSL Rules for 3D-Printed Wall Tiles

## 1. Layout Planning (center-out, balanced cuts, sliver rule, dry layout)

- **Why center-out:** tilers snap chalk lines through the center of the wall/room so all perimeter cuts come out equal on opposing sides and full tiles are maximized. Setting from a corner forces every wall irregularity to compound in one direction toward the most visible edge. Sources: https://www.ceramictilefoundation.org/blog/tile-layout-centered-balanced-no-small-cuts, https://www.stoneworld.com/articles/95159-tile-layout-without-sliver-cuts
- **The sliver rule is the half-tile rule:** industry practice is that no cut tile should be narrower than **1/2 of a full tile**. If a centered layout produces cuts < 1/2 tile at the edges, shift the centerline by half a tile toward one wall ("quartering" — center a grout joint instead of a tile center), which converts two slivers into two ~3/4 cuts. Sources: https://www.diytileguy.com/quartering-tile-layout/, https://www.ceramictilefoundation.org/blog/tile-layout-centered-balanced-no-small-cuts
- **Why slivers are bad beyond aesthetics:** a sliver puts a grout line directly against the wall, so any wall waviness/out-of-plumb reads instantly; a ≥1/2-tile cut moves the joint away from the wall and visually absorbs the error. Source: https://www.stoneworld.com/articles/95159-tile-layout-without-sliver-cuts
- **Dry-layout practice:** lay one row in each direction without adhesive from the center point, **with spacers in place** so the dry joints match real joints; verify squareness with 3-4-5 triangle on chalk lines; use a **story pole** (a stick pre-marked with tile+joint modules) to transfer the layout to walls and predict every cut before setting. Sources: https://www.tilecoach.com/post/how-to-tile-a-shower-wall-like-a-pro-full-guide, https://www.lowes.com/n/how-to/install-floor-tile, https://www.thisoldhouse.com/flooring/23006801/tile-setting
- **Focal-point rule:** the wall facing the doorway / the most-seen plane gets the centered pattern and best cuts; cut rows go where the eye doesn't (back row, bottom row, behind fixtures). Source: https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/tiling/floor-layout

**→ DSL implication:** wall-layout solver anchors the grid at the wall centroid and auto-tries the half-module offset in each axis ("quartering pass"), choosing the layout minimizing fragments; **validator: ERROR if any crop fragment < 0.5 × module width on a sightline edge, WARN if < 0.5 anywhere, allow override**; a `story_pole()` output artifact prints a 1:1 paper/PLA strip of the module pitch for on-wall dry layout; `focal_edge` attribute on wall regions steers where fragment rows land.

## 2. Gaps and Movement

- **Grout joint sizing rule (the key formula):** ANSI A108.02 §4.3.8 — joint width must be **≥ 3× the actual facial-dimension variation** of the tiles supplied, and **never < 1/16" (1.6 mm)**; running-bond with any side >15": ≥1/8" (3.2 mm) for rectified, ≥3/16" (4.8 mm) for calibrated tile, **plus** the tile's longest-edge warpage (e.g., 1/8" + 1/32" warp = 5/32"). Gaps exist to absorb tile size tolerance, substrate irregularity, and angular/warpage error — not for looks. Sources: https://www.ceramictilefoundation.org/blog/offsets-grout-joints-customer-expectations, https://ctasc.com/expert-answers/what-is-the-allowable-tolerance-for-tile-grout-head-joint-widths/, https://www.tileletter.com/about-grout-joint-width/
- **Movement joints (TCNA EJ171):** interior fields need soft movement joints at **max 25 ft (7.6 m)** each direction (20 ft in some conditions); exterior or sun/moisture-exposed interior: **every 8–12 ft (2.4–3.7 m)**; plus **perimeter joints** wherever tile meets a restraining surface, and joints directly over any substrate control joint. Sources: https://www.constructionspecifier.com/specifying-movement-joints-and-sealants-for-tile-and-stone-reviewing-current-industry-standards-and-design-options/, https://cromexinspections.com/article/understanding-tcna-ej171-movement-joints/, https://ctasc.com/expert-answers/how-should-movement-joints-expansion-joints-be-installed-in-tile-installations/
- **PLA vs ceramic movement:** PLA CTE ≈ **70–80 ppm/K** in the glassy state vs ceramic/porcelain **~4–14 ppm/K** — roughly **10× more movement**. Sources: https://www.e3s-conferences.org/articles/e3sconf/abs/2018/07/e3sconf_eenviro2018_01007.html, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9370745/, https://global.kyocera.com/prdct/fc/material-property/property/cte/index.html
  - Worked number: a 100 mm PLA tile over a 20 °C swing grows ~**0.14 mm**; a rigidly-linked 3 m run (30 tiles) accumulates **~4 mm** — the direct cause of the #1 complaint about interlocking garage tiles: **thermal buckling in direct sun**, which is why they mandate **1/4"–1/2" (6–13 mm) perimeter expansion gaps** as floating floors. Sources: https://www.garageflooringllc.com/expansion-and-contraction/, https://allgaragefloors.com/interlocking-garage-floor-tile/
- **Heat thresholds:** PLA Tg **55–65 °C** (permanent creep/sag above), creep already accelerates when ambient exceeds **~40 °C**; PETG Tg **75–85 °C**, dimensionally stable below ~60 °C. Wall surfaces above radiators and behind sunlit glass reach these ranges. Sources: https://www.snapmaker.com/blog/petg-glass-transition-temperature/, https://www.rapid-protos.com/pla-melting-point/, https://qidi3d.com/blogs/print-lab/pla-petg-heavy-wall-hook-material-guide

**→ DSL implication:** translate ANSI's 3× rule as **`joint_gap ≥ 3 × printer_profile.dimensional_tolerance`** (±0.1 mm printer → ≥0.3 mm designed gap) plus a CTE term `α × module × ΔT_design`; validator: **ERROR when a rigidly-clipped run exceeds N modules (default ~10 tiles / 1 m) without a compliant "movement clip" or free joint**, and require `perimeter_clearance ≥ 5 mm` from walls/ceiling/trim; `environment: near_radiator|sunlit` gate: PLA forbidden (or WARN + require PETG/annealed PLA) when `ambient_max ≥ 40 °C`.

## 3. Holes and Cuts

- **Drill-distance rule:** keep any hole **≥ 1/2" (12.7 mm)** from a tile edge, ideally **20–25 mm (3/4–1")**, best near tile center — edges and corners are the crack-initiation zones; techniques (masking tape, water cooling, light pressure, no hammer mode) all exist to prevent starting a crack that then propagates through the brittle field. Sources: https://mybuildingshop.com/blogs/mosaic-tile-news/can-i-drill-a-hole-in-ceramic-tile-without-cracking, https://kuishi.com/en-us/blogs/guides/how-to-drill-into-bathroom-tiles-without-damaging-them, https://www.tilemountain.co.uk/blog/how-to-drill-through-tiles/
- **Cut-edge placement:** cut edges go to the back row against the wall (backsplash rule: "set full tiles from trim forward; all cuts land on the back row"), under trim/cove base, and away from focal sightlines; full tiles at doorways/most-visible planes. Sources: https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/tiling/floor-layout, https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1996/7489/files/Wall_Tile_Installation_Guide___Daltile.pdf
- **Diagonal/herringbone edge handling and waste:** straight lay ~10% waste; diagonal ~15%; herringbone **15–20%** — every perimeter tile takes a 45° cut, and the triangular offcuts are **mirror images** (a left-cut offcut cannot be flipped to fill a right-cut spot, because the finished face flips). Orienting the herringbone V along the long axis drops waste to 12–15%. Sources: https://makecalcs.net/blog/tile-waste-factor-guide, https://tileprocalculator.com/patterns/herringbone-tile-calculator, https://herron.app/guides/herringbone-layout-calculator

**→ DSL implication:** any mounting hole/keyhole/recess feature must keep **≥ 6 mm solid margin** from the tile edge AND from any lattice void edge (printed analog of the 1/2" drill rule — the relief lattice makes local "edges" everywhere); `cut_edge_policy: toward_perimeter | under_trim` steers fragment placement; diagonal layout mode must report **waste % and a mirrored-offcut reuse map** (chirality-aware: a relief pattern is generally not mirror-symmetric, so offcut reuse must check pattern symmetry group).

## 4. Flatness and Lippage (exhaustive — closest analog to our corner clips)

- **Lippage** = vertical offset between adjacent tile faces. ANSI A108.02 allowables: joints 1/16"–<1/4" wide → **1/32" (0.8 mm) + inherent tile warpage**; joints ≥1/4" → **1/16" (1.6 mm) + warpage**. ANSI A137.1 allows inherent warpage of ~1/32"–3/32" depending on tile class — i.e., wider joints buy lippage tolerance. Sources: https://ctasc.com/expert-answers/what-is-the-acceptable-lippage-in-a-tile-installation/, https://www.constructionspecifier.com/tile-and-stone-lippage-what-is-acceptable-and-how-do-you-specify-it/, https://tcnatile.com/resource-center/faq/lippage/
- **Substrate flatness standards:** general tile: **1/4" in 10 ft**; large-format tile (any edge ≥15"): **1/8" in 10 ft and 1/16" in 2 ft**, measured from high points; some mortar manufacturers push 1/16" in 10 ft for ≥16" tile. Sources: https://www.ceramictilefoundation.org/blog/floor-or-wall-flat-enough-for-large-format-tile, https://www.custombuildingproducts.com/media/59601245/slu_wp122.pdf
- **How leveling clip/wedge systems register faces flush (the mechanism to copy):**
  1. A **base plate sits under BOTH adjacent tiles**, with a thin breakaway strap rising through the joint (strap thickness doubles as the spacer: 1/16", 1/8", 3/16").
  2. A **wedge or threaded cap bears on the TOP faces of both tiles simultaneously** and tensions the strap — so both visible faces are clamped against one rigid bridge spanning the joint. Registration is **front-face-to-front-face**, independent of substrate or tile-back irregularity. That is the whole trick.
  3. After cure the strap snaps off at a designed weak point **below finished tile height**.
  Five mechanism families: wedge, spinner (threaded cap), tensioning cap, all-in-one, reusable metal-base. Sources: https://www.diytileguy.com/tile-leveling-systems/, https://www.tilingsuppliesdirect.co.uk/product/clip-wedge-tile-levelling-system, https://www.defusco.com/porcelain-tile-leveling-system/
- **Reported failure modes:** straps not breaking cleanly (protruding stubs); base plates too thick to slide under; **tile cracking under unsupported corners when clamped**; over-tension chipping edges; scratching polished faces. Source: https://www.diytileguy.com/tile-leveling-systems/

**→ DSL implication (multiple):**
- **Corner clips must register the FRONT faces**, not the backs: the clip's bridge should bear on a designed **flat border band** on the tile face; validator: ERROR if the relief lattice leaves no continuous flat band ≥ `clip_bridge_width` (e.g., 4 mm) along each edge/corner where a clip lands.
- `lippage_budget` parameter: clip engagement depth must exceed (tile bow from bed warp, typically 0.2–0.5 mm on 100 mm FDM parts) + first-layer thickness variance; compile-time check `clip_capture ≥ 2 × expected_warp`.
- Wall-flatness input (`substrate_flatness = 1/8_in_per_10ft` default) drives whether rigid back-clips are allowed or compliant/gap-tolerant clips are required.
- Clamping features must not land on unsupported spans of the back-face recess (the "cracked corner" failure): recess floors under clip loads need `min_thickness ≥ 3 perimeters`.

## 5. Mechanical Wall Mounting

- **French cleats / z-clips:** aluminum cleat pairs rated **80–240 lb by length (12"–41")**, heavy-duty versions to **500 lb**; pre-punched for **#8 screws at 8" (203 mm) intervals**. Z-clip panel mounting: two clips on wall opening-up, two on panel opening-down, lift-and-drop. Sources: https://www.amazon.com/Aluminum-500-Pound-Mounting-Interlocking-Z-Hangers/dp/B09NBDW2RN, https://www.lowes.com/pd/Unique-Bargains-French-Cleat-Picture-Hanger-30-Inch-1-Pairs-Aluminum-Z-Clip-Supports-120bs-Interlocking-Wall-Mounting-Bracket-to-Hang-Mirrors-Panels-Shelves-Headboards-Art-Frames/8003401
- **Acoustic-panel practice:** z-clips need **3/4" (19 mm) clearance above the panel** to engage; impaling clips placed **4" inside panel perimeter** plus water-based adhesive in a perimeter bead 2" from edges + center X; 24 h cure. Sources: https://www.acousticdesignworks.com/blogs/research/adw-z-clip-mounting-instructions-for-acoustic-panels, https://acousticalsolutions.com/how-to-install-acoustic-panels, https://www.soundproofcow.com/types-acoustic-panel-mounting-hardware/
- **Drywall anchors by type (shear ratings):** plastic expansion **5–20 lb**; self-drilling **25–75 lb**; molly **25–50 lb**; toggle **75–150 lb**; derate **30–50% for tension/pull-out** loads (a clip cantilever pulls out, not down). Sources: https://www.austingallery.org/blog/how-much-weight-can-drywall-hold, https://www.familyhandyman.com/article/drywall-anchors/, https://betterhomepro.com/drywall-anchors-weight-ratings-types/
- **Keyhole-slot dimensions (printed, #8 screw class):** entry hole **⌀8 mm**, slot **4 mm wide × 8 mm long**, head cavity **~3 mm deep**; #8 heads are a tight fit at these dims — size up slightly. Design law: entry hole > head ⌀; slot width > shank ⌀ but < head ⌀. CNC/woodwork standard uses a 3/8" (9.5 mm) entry. Sources: https://www.printables.com/model/146312-keyhole-slot-for-wall-hanging, https://help.idealinc.com/article/824-what-is-the-keyhole-mounting-hole-dimensions, https://www.rockler.com/hanging-slot-router-bits-router-bits
- Scale check: a 100 mm PLA tile is ~30–80 g (a comparable Printables hex tile is 33 g — https://www.printables.com/model/375786-hexa-decoration-wall-tile); even a 3 m² field is ~10–20 kg total, so per-anchor load is trivial — **anchor count is driven by field stiffness and pull-out from clip moments, not weight**. Command strips (1–3 lb each) are viable per-tile but are the most-reported adhesive failure path.

**→ DSL implication:** `mount = keyhole(screw="#8")` emits a parametric keyhole (entry ⌀ = head ⌀ + 1 mm, slot = shank ⌀ + 0.5 mm, cavity = head height + 0.5 mm, **≥4 mm material collar** behind, ≥6 mm from tile/lattice edges); mounting validator computes field weight, applies **2–3× safety factor and 30–50% tension derate**, and errors if `anchors × derated_rating < demand`; support `cleat_row()` for panelized clusters with the 19 mm top-clearance check baked in.

## 6. Interlocking Panel Products (exhaustive)

- **Garage floor tiles (RaceDeck / Swisstrax):** peg-into-loop snap-lock edges; RaceDeck uses **one robust interlock per side region** with deliberate flex "allowing slight movement for expansion/contraction"; Swisstrax uses **more connections per side** for a tighter, stiffer field. Broken connections are rare in reviews; the dominant field failure is **thermal expansion buckling** — solid-top tiles in direct sun expand faster than the shaded field and, without the mandated **1/4–1/2" perimeter gap**, the floating floor runs out of room and tents. Vented/open tiles suffer less (less absorbing area). Sources: https://allgaragefloors.com/racedeck-vs-swisstrax/, https://www.garageflooringllc.com/expansion-and-contraction/, https://allgaragefloors.com/interlocking-garage-floor-tile/
  - Design lesson: the loop-and-peg is intentionally a **loose-tolerance, compliant joint** — it locates tiles but permits ~fraction-of-mm relative slide, which is what lets a plastic field survive thermal cycling.
- **Acoustic wall panels:** z-clips (mechanical, removable, need top clearance), impaling clips + adhesive (permanent), adhesive-only (failure-prone). Source: https://www.soundproofcow.com/types-acoustic-panel-mounting-hardware/
- **LEGO (the precision ceiling):** clutch is an interference fit of ⌀4.8 mm studs against inner walls/tubes; molds held to **±0.01 mm**; a part **0.02 mm oversize won't assemble, 0.02 mm undersize falls apart**. Sources: https://www.thewave.engineer/articles.html/productivity/legos-0002mm-specification-and-its-implications-for-manufacturing-r120/, https://brickarchitect.com/parts/category-1
  - Lesson: FDM holds ±0.1–0.2 mm at best, 10–20× worse than clutch fit demands — **raw interference fits cannot be the retention mechanism between printed tiles**; retention must come from compliant spring geometry (snap arms, flexing loops) or separate clip parts that can be reprinted at adjusted tolerance.
- **Multiboard / Honeycomb Storage Wall (real shipping 3D-printed wall ecosystems):** connectors designed around a **0.25 mm default tolerance**, and — critically — the ecosystem **publishes user-adjustable tolerance offsets** because every printer differs; boards screw to walls through dedicated holes; "weight-bearing snaps" are a distinct part class from positioning snaps. Sources: https://makerworld.com/en/models/978819-clip-multiboard-to-honeycomb-connector, https://thangs.com/designer/Multiboard/3d-model/Honeycomb%20to%20Multiboard%20Weight%20Bearing%20Snap-1060980, http://www.multiboard.io/
- **Printables/Thingiverse modular wall tiles:** hex wallpanels connect by friction-fit edge frames ("no glue needed when your printer is calibrated"), mounted via command strips or double-sided tape; magnet variants exist (magnet press pockets at **−0.1 mm interference** + glue is the standard recipe). Sources: https://www.printables.com/model/206002-wallpanel-modular-hexagon-tiles, https://www.printables.com/model/850069-magnetic-knob-wall, https://tools.creative3dp.com/blog/press-fit-tolerances-3d-printing/
- **Failure modes across reviews:** thermal buckling (garage tiles); adhesive letdown / command-strip overload (printed hex tiles, 1–3 lb per strip); calibration-dependent fit — the same STL is loose on one printer, unassemblable on another (why Multiboard ships tolerance sliders); brittle snap tabs shearing in PLA; tight fields with no compliance transmitting substrate movement into joint stress.

**→ DSL implication:** connector library as first-class DSL types — `snap_cantilever`, `pin_loop` (compliant, garage-tile style, for field joints), `dovetail_slide`, `magnet_pocket(d, h)` (auto −0.1 mm), `corner_clip` (leveling-system style face-registering); **every connector exposes a `tolerance` parameter with a per-printer calibration offset, Multiboard-style**, rather than baking one value in; validator: field-interior joints must use compliant connector types (movement), only perimeter/mount joints may be rigid; separate `load_bearing: true` connector class with thicker sections.

## 7. 3D-Print Specific (exhaustive)

- **Fit ladder for rigid filaments (PLA/PETG/ABS, 5–25 mm features), designed diametral gap:** press/interference **−0.10 mm**; snug/transition **+0.05 mm**; close running **+0.15 mm**; free/must-work-first-try **+0.35 mm**. What governs the choice: **holes print 0.1–0.3 mm undersize** (slicer polygonization, extrusion overshoot on curves, cooling shrinkage — ~0.24 mm undersize on a 5 mm PLA hole, 0.4 mm nozzle) while **external dims print ~0.1 mm oversize**; PETG's tacky surface eats ~0.05 mm of running clearance (use +0.3 mm total); TPU needs 2× interference and 0.5–0.8 mm running clearance. Prefer **crush ribs (~0.2 mm proud) inside holes** over shrinking the bore. Source: https://tools.creative3dp.com/blog/press-fit-tolerances-3d-printing/, https://zbotic.in/3d-printing-tolerances-designing-gaps-for-press-fits-threads-and-snap-fits/, https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing/
- **Dovetails/alignment joinery:** **0.2–0.5 mm clearance per side** depending on material; Multiboard's shipping default of 0.25 mm sits mid-range; always print a **0.1–0.5 mm step tolerance coupon** per printer+filament before committing a batch. Sources: https://zbotic.in/3d-printing-tolerances-designing-gaps-for-press-fits-threads-and-snap-fits/, https://www.snapmaker.com/blog/3d-printing-tolerances/
- **Snap-fit cantilever rules:** PLA is the **worst** common material for snaps (brittle, snaps at the root); PETG/ABS/nylon preferred — PETG has ~**30% stronger interlayer bonding** than PLA. Geometry: arms **1.2–2× molded-part thickness**, **taper to 50% thickness at tip** (2 mm root → 1 mm tip), **root fillet ≥ 0.5× thickness** (2 mm beam → ≥1 mm fillet), 0.2–0.3 mm clearance around the arm, +0.1–0.2 mm in the catch groove; **print the arm lying in the XY plane so layer lines run along its length** — a vertically printed snap arm delaminates at the first flex. Sources: https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing/, https://www.printpool.co.uk/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-joints-for-3d-printing, https://qidi3d.com/blogs/print-lab/pla-petg-heavy-wall-hook-material-guide
- **Creep under sustained wall load:** PLA fares worst of common filaments for creep; polymers creep at room temperature under sufficient stress; **PLA press fits measurably relax over months**; creep accelerates sharply above **~40 °C ambient** and becomes permanent sag above Tg **55–65 °C**; annealing/heat-treatment improves PLA creep resistance; PETG stays stable below ~60 °C but also creeps under constant stress. Practical hook guidance: **2–3× load safety factor, ≥4 perimeters, 40–50% infill** for 5 kg-class loads, **≥4 mm collar around screw holes**, inspect 6-monthly, plan replacement ~2 years under continuous heavy load. Sources: https://www.ryandynamics.com/insight/materialcreep/, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0997753825002475, https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/app.54463, https://qidi3d.com/blogs/print-lab/pla-petg-heavy-wall-hook-material-guide, https://tools.creative3dp.com/blog/press-fit-tolerances-3d-printing/
- **Key synthesis for our clips:** a corner clip that holds tiles by **sustained spring deflection will creep loose in PLA** (and slowly in PETG). Clips should reach a **zero-strain rest state when engaged** (detent past-center, or loaded in shear/bearing like a keyhole or pin-loop) so retention doesn't depend on maintained elastic stress.

**→ DSL implication:** global `printer_profile { hole_compensation, xy_compensation, tolerance_class }` applied at compile time so the DSL stores *intent* fits ("press", "sliding", "free") not raw offsets; built-in `tolerance_coupon()` generator emitting a step-gauge print as the first artifact of any project; material rule matrix at compile time — `PLA + snap_cantilever(sustained_deflection=true)` → ERROR "creep: use zero-preload detent or PETG"; snap-arm macro auto-generates taper/fillet/orientation and refuses arms that would print vertically; per-feature `sustained_stress` flag cross-checked against `ambient_max`.
